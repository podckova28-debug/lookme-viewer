## Title
Why It's So Hard to Be an Underwater Welder

Alternatives: "Why It's So Hard to Be an Underwater Welder (It's Not the 15% Myth)" · "Underwater Welder: The Job Nobody Sees"

## Description
Sixty feet under the Gulf of Mexico, it is so dark you can't see your own glove. It is cold, it is loud, and you are about to start a fire. Underwater.

From dive school and life as a tender, to the first dive, wet welding, Delta P, the bends and 28 days of saturation in a steel tube: what it is really like to be an underwater welder, and why the famous "15% of them die" is a myth (the real number is still frightening).

The diver and everyone in this video are fictional illustrations; the facts and numbers come from the sources below.

Sources:
• Commercial diving school: about 2–7 months; tuition often $15,000–$24,000, plus room, board and gear — Dive Training Magazine; Divers Institute of Technology
• Graduates start as tenders (ADCI Entry Level Tender/Diver)
• Hot-water suits and surface-supplied umbilicals; "make it hot / make it cold" power commands; near-zero visibility, work by touch — AWS Welding Digest (underwater welding safety); Fronius; Kemppi
• Pressure doubles at 33 feet of seawater — basic diving physics
• Wet welds cool fast and can be brittle; critical welds often done dry in a hyperbaric habitat — AWS D3.6M; Fronius
• Commercial diving fatality rate: 158.9 per 100,000 workers per year (2011–2017) — CDC/NIOSH, Maritime Safety and Health (BLS CFOI data)
• Alaska 1990–1997: about 180 per 100,000, 40 times the national average for all workers — CDC MMWR 1998;47(22):452–455
• Delta P (differential pressure) hazards and pre-dive checks — ADCI; HSE
• Saturation: normally no more than 28 days — DMAC 21; decompression from ~650 ft about 8 days — Divers Alert Network (DAN)
• Median annual wage for commercial divers about $61,300 (May 2023) — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, OEWS 49-9092
