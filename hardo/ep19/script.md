## COLD OPEN
1. By twenty thirty-five, America is expected to have almost six million more jobs than today. But some jobs are going the other way. Fast. A few could lose a third of their workers in just ten years.
2. Here are ten jobs that are disappearing fastest, ranked by the official projections of the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Not overnight. But year by year, there will be fewer of them.
## #10 — SEWING MACHINE OPERATOR (−15.1%)
3. Number ten. The sewing machine operator. About a hundred and seventeen thousand Americans still sew clothes, bags and furniture covers in factories.
4. By twenty thirty-five, that number is expected to drop by about fifteen percent. Most clothing is now made overseas, and machines handle more of the simple seams.
## #9 — CAMERA REPAIRER (−15.5%)
5. Number nine. The camera repairer. There are fewer than two thousand of them left in the whole country.
6. And they are expected to shrink by fifteen and a half percent. Most photos are now taken on phones. And when a camera breaks, people usually just replace it.
## #8 — FILE CLERK (−15.8%)
7. Number eight. The file clerk. Their job is to keep paper records in order, so anyone can find the right folder.
8. But paper is moving into computers. A search box finds in one second what used to take a clerk ten minutes. Expected change: almost sixteen percent fewer file clerks.
## #7 — CARPET INSTALLER (−16.4%)
9. Number seven. The carpet installer. This one is a surprise, because people still buy homes and renovate them.
10. The difference is the floor itself. Many homeowners now choose wood, tile or vinyl instead of wall-to-wall carpet. Expected drop: about sixteen percent.
## #6 — PRINT BINDING WORKER (−17.5%)
11. Number six. The print binding worker. They fold, stitch, glue and trim pages into books, magazines and catalogs.
12. Fewer things are printed today, and modern binding machines need fewer hands. Expected drop: seventeen and a half percent.
## #5 — TELEMARKETER (−21.4%)
13. Number five. The telemarketer. The person who calls you at dinner time to sell you something.
14. Most people no longer answer unknown numbers, and many sales calls are now made by automated systems. About one in five telemarketing jobs is expected to vanish.
## #4 — FOUNDRY MOLD MAKER (−23.0%)
15. Number four. The foundry mold maker. They shape the sand molds that hot liquid metal is poured into, to make engine parts, pipes and machine pieces.
16. More of this work is now done by machines, including printers that print the molds directly. Expected drop: twenty-three percent.
## #3 — DATA ENTRY KEYER (−25.5%)
17. Number three. The data entry keyer. They type information from forms, invoices and records into computers, all day long.
18. Today, scanners and software read most of those forms automatically. That's why a quarter of these jobs are expected to disappear. About thirty-four thousand of them.
## #2 — SWITCHBOARD OPERATOR (−26.0%; telephone operators −27.6%)
19. Number two. The switchboard operator. They answer calls and connect each caller to the right person or department.
20. Now an automated menu does that. Press one for sales, press two for support. Switchboard jobs are expected to fall by twenty-six percent, and classic telephone operators by almost twenty-eight.
## #1 — WORD PROCESSOR AND TYPIST (−34.4%)
21. Number one. The word processor and typist. Their whole job is typing up letters, reports and documents for other people.
22. But today almost everyone types their own emails and documents, and voice typing does the rest. Expected drop: more than thirty-four percent. That's one in every three jobs.
23. From about forty thousand typists today, to about twenty-six thousand in twenty thirty-five.
## THE PATTERN
24. So what do these jobs have in common? Most of them repeat one task all day: typing, filing, connecting calls, sewing the same seam. And a repeated task is exactly what machines and software do best.
25. But notice what is not happening. These jobs are not vanishing overnight. Tens of thousands of people still do them today. And their skills, like precision, patience and dealing with people, carry over to new jobs.
26. So if your job is on this list, it's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to learn the next skill a little earlier than everyone else.
## OUTRO
27. Which of these jobs did you think had already disappeared? Tell us in the comments. And if you liked this, subscribe. Next time: the new jobs that will appear in the next ten years.
