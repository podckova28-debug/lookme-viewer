## Title

Alternatives: "10 Deadliest Jobs in America, Ranked" · "The #1 Deadliest Job Is 30x Riskier Than Yours"

## Description
In 2024, more than 5,000 Americans went to work and never came home. But the risk is not spread evenly: in some jobs it is more than thirty times higher than average.

The ten most dangerous jobs in America, ranked by real fatal injury rates (deaths per 100,000 full-time workers): grounds maintenance workers and tree trimmers, truck drivers, underground mining machine operators, construction helpers, pilots, garbage collectors, ironworkers, roofers, commercial fishermen and loggers. And what they have in common: heights, heavy machines, moving vehicles, water, falling trees and working far from help.

All rates are U.S. figures for 2024 from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Everyone in this video is a fictional illustration.

Sources:
• U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries, hours-based fatal injury rates per 100,000 FTE workers, 2024 (released February 2026): all workers 3.3; logging workers 110.4; fishing and hunting workers 88.8; roofers 48.7; structural iron and steel workers 37.8; refuse and recyclable material collectors 37.4; aircraft pilots and flight engineers 36.7; construction trades helpers 35.8; underground mining machine operators 35.6; driver/sales workers and truck drivers 25.7; grounds maintenance workers 20.9; firefighters 4.0; protective service occupations 8.5
• BLS, Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries Summary, 2024 (USDL-26-0230, 19 Feb 2026): 5,070 fatal work injuries; heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers 798 deaths, the most of any occupation
