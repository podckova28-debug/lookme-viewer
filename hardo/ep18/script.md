## COLD OPEN
1. In 2024, more than five thousand Americans went to work and never came home. But the risk is not spread evenly. In some jobs, it's more than thirty times higher than average.
2. Here are the ten most dangerous jobs in America, ranked by real death rates from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. For the average worker, the rate is three point three deaths per hundred thousand. Keep that number in mind.
## #10 — TREE TRIMMER AND GROUNDS WORKER
3. Number ten. Grounds maintenance workers. That's landscapers, gardeners, and the people who climb and cut trees. Their rate: about twenty-one deaths per hundred thousand.
4. The danger isn't the lawn mower. It's the tree. Climbers fall, cut branches swing the wrong way, and a chainsaw high up leaves no room for mistakes.
## #9 — TRUCK DRIVER
5. Number nine. The truck driver. The rate is about twenty-six per hundred thousand. But this job has something no other job on this list has.
6. More truck drivers die at work than people in any other single job. Almost eight hundred heavy truck drivers in 2024. Long hours, tired drivers, and every day on the highway.
## #8 — UNDERGROUND MINER
7. Number eight. The underground mining machine operator. They drive the huge machines that cut coal and rock deep below the surface.
8. Their rate: about thirty-six per hundred thousand. Underground, the dangers are heavy machines in tight tunnels, falling rock, and very few ways out.
## #7 — CONSTRUCTION HELPER
9. Number seven. The construction helper. They carry, lift, hold and clean up for the skilled trades. Often it's someone's first job on a building site.
10. And that's part of the problem. New workers are still learning where the danger is. Their rate is almost thirty-six per hundred thousand, more than ten times the average.
## #6 — PILOT
11. Number six. The pilot. This one surprises people, because flying on an airliner is one of the safest ways to travel.
12. Most of the risk is in small planes: crop dusters, bush pilots, air taxis and small cargo flights, often in bad weather and remote places. The rate for pilots: about thirty-seven per hundred thousand.
## #5 — GARBAGE COLLECTOR
13. Number five. The garbage collector. You see them every week, and probably never think of it as dangerous.
14. But their rate is about thirty-seven per hundred thousand. They work on the road, early in the morning, often in the dark, right next to passing traffic and their own heavy truck.
## #4 — IRONWORKER
15. Number four. The ironworker. They build the steel skeletons of bridges and skyscrapers, walking on narrow beams high above the ground.
16. Their rate: about thirty-eight per hundred thousand. They wear harnesses, but one slip, one gust of wind, one loose beam, can still be fatal.
## #3 — ROOFER
17. Number three. The roofer. Every house needs a roof, and someone has to stand on it, on a slope, in the sun or the wind, carrying heavy bundles.
18. The rate jumps here: almost forty-nine per hundred thousand. That's about fifteen times the average job. And the main killer is simple. Falls.
## #2 — FISHERMAN
19. Number two. The commercial fisherman. Out at sea, far from help, in cold water, with heavy gear and a deck that never stops moving.
20. Their rate is about eighty-nine per hundred thousand. When something goes wrong out there, a boat can sink fast, and the nearest rescue can be hours away.
## #1 — LOGGER
21. Number one. The logger. They cut down huge trees in steep forests, often far from any road or hospital.
22. Their rate: about one hundred ten deaths per hundred thousand. That's more than thirty times the average worker. And most of those deaths come from the same thing: a falling tree, or a log that rolls the wrong way.
23. Think about that. A job where the most dangerous moment is the very thing you came to do.
## THE PATTERN
24. So what do these jobs have in common? Heights. Heavy machines. Moving vehicles. Water. Falling trees. And very often, working far from help.
25. And notice what is not on the list. Firefighters face real danger, but their death rate is about four per hundred thousand. Police and other protective jobs come in around eight and a half. The deadliest jobs are often the ones we barely notice.
26. The good news: safety rules, harnesses and better equipment have saved many lives. But next time you see someone on a roof, on a garbage truck, or up a tree, remember. Their workday is a lot riskier than yours.
