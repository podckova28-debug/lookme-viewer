# 10 Jobs AI Can't Replace — Episode 15 (~6 min, 27 blocks)

Style: STYLE.md (cook look). Narrator: free OmniVoice clone of Grady (speed 0.85). Format: countdown listicle. Angle (user): who is impossible to replace — hands, unpredictable places, trust.
Cast (fictional): roofer, massage therapist, plumber, electrician, wind turbine technician, hazmat worker (sheet 1); phlebotomist, funeral director/embalmer, nurse practitioner, home health aide, elderly client, a generic white service robot (sheet 2). Hans Moravec is named, not drawn.

## COLD OPEN
1. AI can now write essays, draw pictures and translate languages in seconds. So millions of people are asking a very reasonable question: is my job next?
2. Let's flip it. Here are ten jobs AI can barely touch, counted down, based on real research. Some of them pay better than you'd think. And one of them, you've probably never thought about.
## #10 — ROOFER
3. Number ten. The roofer. In twenty twenty-five, Microsoft researchers looked at two hundred thousand real conversations with their AI assistant, and measured which jobs it could actually help with. Roofers landed near the very bottom.
4. It makes sense. Every roof is different. It's steep, it's wet, it's windy, and the work is done by hand, thirty feet up. A chatbot can explain how shingles work. It cannot climb a ladder.
## #9 — MASSAGE THERAPIST
5. Number nine. The massage therapist. Same study, same result. The whole job is hands, pressure, and feeling what the body is doing, muscle by muscle.
6. AI can guess where your back hurts, if you describe it well. But it can't feel the knot. And most people would rather not be massaged by a robot arm anyway.
## #8 — PLUMBER
7. Number eight. The plumber. Every house is a puzzle: old pipes, odd angles, a leak somewhere behind a wall. You work on your back, under a sink, mostly by feel.
8. Robots are great in a factory, where every part is in the same place every time. A crawl space under a hundred-year-old house is the exact opposite. That's why plumbers keep getting calls.
## #7 — ELECTRICIAN
9. Number seven. The electrician. In the United States, jobs for electricians are projected to grow nine percent by twenty thirty-four. That's about seventy-seven thousand new jobs.
10. And here's the irony: the AI boom needs them. Every new data center, every charging station and every solar roof has to be wired by a human with a licence and steady hands.
## #6 — WIND TURBINE TECHNICIAN
11. Number six. The wind turbine technician. It's one of the fastest growing jobs in America, with employment projected to grow almost fifty percent by twenty thirty-four.
12. The office is a box at the top of a tower, hundreds of feet up, in the wind. You climb, you clip in, and you fix a machine the size of a bus. No software update does that for you.
## #5 — HAZARDOUS MATERIALS REMOVAL WORKER
13. Number five. The hazardous materials removal worker. Asbestos, lead, toxic waste. Somebody has to suit up, go in, and carefully take it out.
14. In the Microsoft study, this job ranked among the ten least touched by AI. Every building is different, every mistake is dangerous, and the rules are strict for a reason.
## #4 — PHLEBOTOMIST
15. Number four. The phlebotomist. That's the person who draws your blood. In the Microsoft study, it came out as the job AI touches least of all.
16. Finding a vein is a skill. Calming a nervous patient, or a crying five-year-old, is another one. Doing both in under a minute is something no machine does yet.
## #3 — EMBALMER
17. Number three. The embalmer. It's a quiet job most people never think about. Embalmers prepare the dead, so families can say goodbye.
18. It takes skill, steady hands and a lot of respect. It also made Microsoft's list of the jobs least affected by AI. Some work is simply too human to hand over.
## #2 — NURSE PRACTITIONER
19. Number two. The nurse practitioner. These are advanced nurses who can examine, diagnose and treat patients. It's the fastest growing healthcare job in the United States, projected to grow about forty percent by twenty thirty-four.
20. AI will help them, by writing notes, checking scans and flagging risks. But someone still has to sit with the patient, notice what's wrong, and be trusted with the decision.
## #1 — HOME HEALTH AIDE
21. Number one. The home health aide. These workers help older and sick people at home: getting up, washing, eating, taking medicine. They are the human side of care.
22. In the United States, this job is projected to add about seven hundred and forty thousand jobs by twenty thirty-four. That's more than any other occupation. And nursing assistants ranked second in Microsoft's list of jobs least affected by AI.
## WHO IS MOST EXPOSED
23. So which jobs are most exposed? In the same study: interpreters and translators, historians, writers, and customer service. And the World Economic Forum expects bank tellers, data entry clerks and postal clerks to shrink the most.
24. That doesn't mean those people simply disappear. It means the tasks change. The World Economic Forum still expects about a hundred and seventy million new jobs worldwide by twenty thirty, against ninety-two million lost.
## WHY THE SAFE JOBS ARE SAFE
25. So why are the safe jobs safe? In the nineteen eighties, a robotics researcher named Hans Moravec noticed something strange. For computers, the hard things, like chess and math, are easy. The easy things, like walking, grabbing and seeing, are incredibly hard.
26. That is still mostly true. The jobs AI can't replace need hands, unpredictable places, and people who trust you. If your work has all three, a robot is not coming for it anytime soon.
