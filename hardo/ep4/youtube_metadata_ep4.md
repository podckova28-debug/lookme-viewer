## Title
Why It's So Hard to Be a Line Cook

## Description
It's 9:14 p.m. on a Saturday. Forty-one tickets on the rail, a burn you don't remember getting, and table twelve wants the salmon medium-well.

This is one day as a line cook, lived from the inside: forty pounds of onions, the walk-in as the only quiet place in the building, a ticket printer that sounds like a machine gun by seven, six pans on four burners, an allergy ticket done right in ninety seconds, the 86 board that doesn't help, and eleven minutes with a mop and the music turned up.

Based on real research:
• The median cook in the US earns $17.19 an hour, about $35,760 a year — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2024 data
• In full-service restaurants, cuts bad enough to miss work happen at 23.6 cases per 10,000 full-time workers vs 7.1 across private industry; thermal burns at 10.2 vs 1.5 — BLS, Injuries, Illnesses, and Fatalities, 2019
• Monthly separations in accommodation and food services ran about 5–6% through 2023–24 vs about 3% for the whole economy — BLS Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey
