## COLD OPEN
1. Before alarm clocks, somebody was paid to wake you up. Before toilets, somebody was paid to empty what was under you. History is full of jobs that make your job look like a holiday.
2. Today: ten of the worst jobs in history, counted down from bad to unbelievable. Every one of them was real, somebody did it every day, and somebody's mother was proud of them. Probably.
## #10 — THE KNOCKER-UPPER
3. Number ten. The knocker-upper. In industrial Britain, factory shifts started before dawn, and almost nobody owned an alarm clock. So you walked the dark streets with a long pole and tapped on bedroom windows until someone waved back.
4. Some knocker-uppers used a pea shooter instead, and fired dried peas at the glass. The pay was a few pence a week per house. And the trade lasted longer than you'd think: the last ones are thought to have retired in the nineteen seventies.
## #9 — THE LEECH COLLECTOR
5. Number nine. The leech collector. In the eighteen hundreds, doctors used leeches for almost everything. Somebody had to catch them. The method was simple. You waded into a cold marsh with bare legs, and waited for them to bite.
6. A good collector could gather up to two thousand five hundred leeches in a day. At the height of the craze, France alone imported more than thirty million leeches in a single year. Many collectors suffered from blood loss and infections.
## #8 — THE MUDLARK
7. Number eight. The mudlark. In Victorian London, when the tide went out, children walked into the stinking mud of the River Thames, often barefoot, looking for anything they could sell.
8. Lumps of coal, old rope, bones, copper nails dropped from ships. A good day might earn a few pennies. The river was also London's sewer. The mudlarks went in anyway, every low tide, in winter too.
## #7 — THE PURE FINDER
9. Number seven. The pure finder. Victorian tanneries needed something special to soften fine leather, like the skins used for gloves and book covers. That something was dog poo. They called it pure.
10. So the pure finders walked the streets with a bucket and a glove, sometimes only one glove, collecting it. Then they sold it to the tanneries by the bucket. Somewhere, a very elegant pair of gloves started its life in that bucket.
## #6 — THE WHIPPING BOY
11. Number six. The whipping boy. The story goes like this: a young prince could not be beaten by his teachers, so when he misbehaved, his friend was punished instead, in front of him.
12. It's one of the most famous jobs in history. There's just one problem. Historians have found very little evidence that it really happened. The worst job on this list might be the one that never existed. Which is honestly the best way to have it.
## #5 — THE CLIMBING BOY
13. Number five. The climbing boy. Chimneys were narrow, so chimney sweeps sent small children up inside them, to scrape the soot off with their hands and a brush, in total darkness.
14. A law in seventeen eighty-eight said they had to be at least eight years old. It was hardly enforced. Boys got stuck, burned and sick. It took until eighteen seventy-five for Britain to finally stop it, by making every sweep get a police licence.
## #4 — THE GONG FARMER
15. Number four. The gong farmer. In Tudor England, toilets emptied into deep pits called gongs. When a pit was full, a gong farmer climbed in and shoveled it out, by hand, into barrels.
16. Because the job was so disgusting, they were only allowed to work at night. One record says gong farmers at a royal palace were paid sixpence a day, which was actually good money. Nobody wanted to know how they earned it.
## #3 — THE SIN EATER
17. Number three. The sin eater. In parts of Wales and western England, folklore says that when someone died, bread and salt were placed on the body. A poor stranger was paid to eat it, and with it, the dead person's sins.
18. Afterwards, the sin eater was shunned, because he now carried everyone else's sins. Historians still argue about how common this really was. But it was written down, more than once. Imagine putting that on a job application.
## #2 — THE GROOM OF THE STOOL
19. Number two. The Groom of the Stool. This one was a royal job. The groom helped the Tudor king on the toilet. Every day. Personally.
20. And here's the twist. It was one of the most powerful jobs at court. The groom was the man closest to the king, and became something like his private secretary. The worst job on paper was the one every nobleman wanted.
## #1 — THE RESURRECTION MAN
21. Number one. The resurrection man. In the seventeen and eighteen hundreds, medical schools needed bodies to study, and there were not enough legal ones. So, at night, men dug up fresh graves and sold the bodies to anatomists.
22. In eighteen twenty-eight, a body could sell for around eight guineas, more than a laborer earned in months. Families guarded graves with iron cages. The trade only faded after the Anatomy Act of eighteen thirty-two gave doctors legal bodies to study.
## BONUS — THE LECTOR
23. And a bonus, because history wasn't all terrible. In Cuban cigar factories, from the eighteen sixties, the workers paid a man to sit on a platform and read to them while they rolled cigars: newspapers, novels, even Shakespeare.
24. He was called the lector. The workers chose the books, and paid him out of their own wages. Some cigars are even named after the novels the lectors read. In Cuba, the job still exists today.
## WHY THESE JOBS EXISTED
25. So what do these jobs have in common? Each one existed because somebody needed a problem solved, and there was no machine to do it yet. No alarm clock. No sewers. No medicine. Just a person, willing to do it.
26. And for most of them, that person was poor, young, or both. The next time your job feels bad, remember: at least nobody asked you to wade into a marsh and wait for the leeches.
