## Title
The Loneliest Jobs on Earth

Alternatives: "10 Loneliest Jobs on Earth (Counted Down)" · "Jobs Where You Don't See Anyone for Months"

## Description
Imagine going to work and not seeing another human face for weeks. For some people, that's the job description.

Ten of the loneliest jobs on Earth, counted down: the lighthouse keeper, the penguin postmaster of Port Lockroy, the fire lookout, the sheepherder, the long-haul trucker, the island weather observer, the saturation diver, the submariner, the South Pole winter crew, and the Concordia crew that space agencies study to prepare for Mars. Plus a bonus from history: the Apollo command module pilot who circled the Moon alone.

Everyone in this video is a fictional illustration; the facts and numbers come from the sources below.

Sources:
• Lighthouse keepers: Trinity House "A fine farewell" (North Foreland, keepers withdrawn November 1998); NPR (Dec 2023) and National Park Service, Boston Light (last official U.S. keeper retired end of 2023)
• Port Lockroy: UK Antarctic Heritage Trust recruitment coverage (Fox Weather 2024): small team, about five months a season, gentoo penguin counts, no running water, tens of thousands of postcards
• Fire lookouts: HistoryLink.org essay 8034 (Jack Kerouac, 63 days on Desolation Peak, 1956); National Forest Foundation "A Room With a View"
• Sheepherders: High Country News; The Journal (Cortez); NPR "Sheepherding remains a lonely life"; Western Horseman on Basque herders ("struck by sagebrush")
• Truck drivers: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (about 2.2 million heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers, 2024)
• Gough Island: ACAP and SANAP (overwintering teams of about 13 months; annual relief voyage of the S.A. Agulhas II)
• Saturation diving: DMAC guidance note 21 (28-day limit); Atlas Obscura, gCaptain (heliox, multi-day decompression)
• Submarines: U.S. Navy fact file, Ballistic Missile Submarines (average patrol about 77 days); The War Horse (familygrams of about 40 words)
• South Pole: NOAA GML (winter crew isolated mid-February to late October); The Antarctic Sun (crews of about 40–48)
• Concordia: European Space Agency (no plane in winter, nearest people 600 km away at Vostok, about four months without sun, down to −80 °C, "White Mars")
• Apollo 11: Michael Collins, Carrying the Fire (1974); NASA commentary "Not since Adam has any human known such solitude"; about 47 minutes without radio contact per orbit
