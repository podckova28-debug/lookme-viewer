# The Weirdest Jobs in the World — Episode 11 (~7 min, 27 blocks)

Style: STYLE.md (cook look: thin line, realistic proportions, soft flat colour, light partial setting fading into white). Narrator: free OmniVoice clone of Grady (speed 0.85, plan tempo 1.0).
Format: a countdown, 10 to 1, plus a bonus. No recurring hero: each job has its own fictional worker, kept consistent across that job's shots (the wide shot is the reference for its close-ups). No real person is drawn or named.

## COLD OPEN
1. Somewhere right now, a person is sniffing an armpit. For money. On purpose. With a clipboard. And they are not the strangest worker on this list.
2. Today: ten real jobs that sound made up. Every one of them exists, somebody is doing it this morning, and somebody's mother is trying to explain it at a family dinner.
## #10 BICYCLE FISHER (Amsterdam)
3. Number ten. The bicycle fisher. You work for the water company in Amsterdam. Your boat has a crane with a big steel claw. You lower it into the canal, close it, lift. Bicycle. Again. Bicycle.
4. Between twelve and fifteen thousand bikes come out of Amsterdam's canals every year. Most were stolen and dumped. Some still have a lock on them, locked to nothing at all. Very secure.
## #9 PROFESSIONAL LINE STANDER (Washington)
5. Number nine. The professional line stander. In Washington, people with no time pay people with time to stand in line for them, often outside congressional hearings, sometimes all night.
6. Clients have paid around forty to sixty dollars an hour. The person actually standing has earned much less. You bring a folding chair, a phone charger and the patience of a statue. At the door, you swap places and go home.
## #8 FORTUNE COOKIE WRITER
7. Number eight. The fortune cookie writer. One of America's biggest fortune cookie makers says it bakes around four million cookies a day. Every single one needs a little sentence about your future.
8. For about thirty years, many of those sentences came from one man, who was also the company's finance chief. Then he stopped. The reason he gave: writer's block. Somewhere, a cookie said: big changes are coming.
## #7 WATER SLIDE TESTER
9. Number seven. The water slide tester. In 2013, a British travel company advertised a six-month job: fly to resorts in Egypt, Majorca and Turkey, ride the slides, and report back.
10. The pay was twenty thousand pounds a year, pro rata. The application was a thirty-second video. The uniform was swim shorts. The hardest part of the job: writing the word splash in a serious tone.
## #6 GOLF BALL DIVER
11. Number six. The golf ball diver. Every pond on a golf course is full of expensive mistakes. You dive in. The water is so murky you work by feel, on your knees in the mud, eight to ten hours a day.
12. The hazards, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: fishing line that can trap you, objects you can't see, and alligators. You're paid per ball. The balls get cleaned and sold again, ready for their next pond.
## #5 ODOR JUDGE
13. Number five. The odor judge. A deodorant company says its product works all day. Somebody has to check. That somebody is you. Volunteers give up their own deodorant for a week. Then they come to the lab.
14. You lean in, you sniff, and you give the armpit a score, often on a scale from one to ten. Then you come back hours later and do it again. Judges are tested for their sense of smell. It is real science. It is also an armpit.
## #4 SNAKE MILKER
15. Number four. The snake milker. At a reptile zoo in Kentucky, venom is collected by hand. You hold the snake's head, it bites a cup covered with a thin film, and the venom runs down the glass.
16. One milker there has said he does about a hundred and fifty snakes in two hours, about a minute each, six hundred to a thousand a week. The venom goes to research labs and into work on antivenom. The snakes are not grateful.
## #3 ICE CREAM TASTER
17. Number three. The ice cream taster. You taste with a gold-plated spoon, so the metal doesn't change the flavor. You swirl, you think, and you spit it out. Swallowing is for amateurs.
18. One taster at an American ice cream company had his taste buds insured for one million dollars. He stayed away from garlic, onions and spicy food. Every dinner was a risk assessment.
## #2 CHICK SEXER
19. Number two. The chick sexer. A newborn chick looks the same whether it will grow into a hen or a rooster. A specialist can tell, by looking very closely at a spot almost nobody wants to look at.
20. The method was developed in Japan in the nineteen twenties and thirties. Training takes years. Top sexers sort around a thousand chicks an hour and aim for ninety-nine percent accuracy. The chicks have no comment.
## #1 TRAIN PUSHER (Tokyo)
21. Number one. The train pusher. In Tokyo's rush hour, the train arrives already full. The doors open. More people get in. And a station attendant in white gloves pushes them, gently and firmly, until the doors can close.
22. In 1975, Tokyo rush-hour trains ran at an average of two hundred and twenty-one percent of their designed capacity. The first pushers at Shinjuku Station were mostly students working part time. The trains are less crowded now. The white gloves are still there.
## BONUS: PROFESSIONAL MOURNER
23. A bonus, because it's older than all the others: the professional mourner. In ancient Egypt, people were paid to weep at funerals. Victorian England hired silent mutes in black to stand by the door.
24. And one British company has offered mourners for hire: around forty-five pounds for two hours, briefed on the life of a person they never met, so they can talk to the family. Somebody has to be good at being sad for strangers.
## WHY
25. So why do these jobs exist? Because every strange job solves a real problem. Bikes block canals. Deodorant claims need proof. Trains have to leave on time. Somebody has to do it.
26. And those somebodies are usually proud of it. Ask a bicycle fisher about the oddest thing in the canal. Ask a chick sexer how fast she is. Ask an odor judge about their day. Actually, maybe don't.
27. If your job sounds normal, enjoy it. If it doesn't, tell us in the comments. And if you liked this, subscribe. Next time: back to the hardest jobs, one day at a time.
