## Title
Why It's So Hard to Be a Truck Driver

## Description
It's 4:50 a.m. Your bed is a shelf behind two seats, your window is a rest area in Nebraska, and a black box on the dashboard has already started counting.

This is one day as a long-haul truck driver, lived from the inside: eighteen tires checked with a flashlight, a seventy-two-foot vehicle, a 7:30 appointment that becomes a 10:15 dock, two hours of unpaid "detention", a GPS that wants you under a twelve-foot bridge, a thirty-minute break that is the only time the clock stops, and fifty-six minutes a day looking for somewhere to park it all.

Based on real research:
• Median pay for heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers: $57,440 a year, $27.62 an hour — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2024
• Hours of service: 11 hours driving, 14-hour window, 30-minute break by hour 8, 60/70 hours in 7/8 days, 34-hour restart — FMCSA, Summary of Hours of Service Regulations
• Detention (waiting beyond two hours at a facility) reported at 39.3% of stops; shippers pay roughly half of detention invoices — American Transportation Research Institute, Costs and Consequences of Truck Driver Detention, 2024
• Drivers spend an average of 56 minutes a day looking for parking, about $4,600 a year in lost wages — ATRI Truck Parking Diary study, 2016
• 823 heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers died on the job in 2023, more than any other single occupation — BLS Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries 2023
• Driver turnover at large truckload carriers averaged roughly 90% a year through the 2010s — American Trucking Associations
