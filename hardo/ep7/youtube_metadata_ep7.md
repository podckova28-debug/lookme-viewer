# Episode 7 — YouTube metadata

## Title
Why It's So Hard to Be a Firefighter

## Description
It's 2:11 a.m. The tones drop. Forty-five seconds later you're on the truck, and your brain still hasn't been consulted.

One shift as a firefighter, lived from the inside: a 24-hour day, the 7 a.m. rig check, seventy pounds of gear, the call nobody expects (most of them are medical), a smoke alarm with a very loud dog, chili that's cold by four, a real fire at 3:40 p.m., what the smoke costs your body, the pay, the volunteers, one ear open at night, and a dumpster with a raccoon watching.

Based on real research:
• Median pay for firefighters: $59,530 a year; about 345,000 jobs — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2024
• 36.6 million fire department calls in 2021; about 1.35 million were fires (roughly 1 in 27) and 72% were medical — NFPA, Fire Department Calls
• Occupational exposure as a firefighter classified as Group 1, carcinogenic to humans — IARC Monographs vol. 132, 2022
• About 9% more cancer diagnoses and 14% more cancer deaths than the general US population, cohort of about 30,000 firefighters — NIOSH (Daniels et al.)
• About two in three US firefighters are volunteers — National Volunteer Fire Council / NFPA
• 24/48 and 48/96 shift schedules, about 56 hours a week — IAFF shift-work material
Gear weight and turnout time are rules of thumb; the sleep comparison refers to fatigue studies (Dawson & Reid, 1997).
