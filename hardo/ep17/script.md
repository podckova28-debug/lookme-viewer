# Jobs That Pay Insanely Well (But Nobody Wants Them) — Episode 17 (~6.5 min, 27 blocks)

## COLD OPEN
1. Some jobs pay close to a hundred and fifty thousand dollars a year, and they still can't find enough people. Why? Because of what you have to do to earn it.
2. Here are ten jobs that pay surprisingly well, but most people would never take. All numbers are American median pay, from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. And number one pays almost three times the typical job.
## #10 — WASTEWATER PLANT OPERATOR
3. Number ten. The wastewater plant operator. Everything you flush has to go somewhere. These operators keep the plant running, so the dirty water comes out clean.
4. The median pay is about sixty thousand dollars a year. The catch: the smell, the noise, dangerous gases, and a plant that never stops. Nights, weekends and holidays included.
## #9 — IRONWORKER
5. Number nine. The ironworker. These are the people who walk on narrow steel beams, sometimes fifty stories up, bolting skyscrapers together.
6. Median pay: around sixty-three thousand dollars. They wear harnesses, but a fall from that height can still be deadly. And when it's icy or very windy, the work has to stop.
## #8 — RIG DRILL OPERATOR
7. Number eight. The oil rig drill operator. They run the drill that bores deep into the ground for oil and gas, on land or far out at sea.
8. Median pay: about sixty-eight thousand. The downside: offshore and remote crews can be away for weeks at a time, working long shifts seven days a week, in the noise and in any weather.
## #7 — ROOF BOLTER
9. Number seven. The roof bolter. In an underground mine, before anyone else can work, somebody has to stop the ceiling from falling. They drill long steel bolts into the rock above their heads.
10. They earn around seventy-five thousand dollars a year. In the dark, in the dust, far below the surface, with the whole mountain sitting on top of them.
## #6 — LOCOMOTIVE ENGINEER
11. Number six. The locomotive engineer. They drive freight trains, some of them more than a mile long, across the country.
12. Median pay: about eighty-one thousand. But trains run day and night. Schedules can be irregular, long routes keep you away from home, and the best shifts go to whoever has the most seniority.
## #5 — SHIP CAPTAIN
13. Number five. The ship captain. Captains and mates steer barges, tankers and cargo ships across lakes, rivers and oceans.
14. Median pay: about ninety-two thousand dollars. The catch: weeks or even months away from home, working seven days a week on board, in any weather the sea decides to send.
## #4 — POWER LINE WORKER
15. Number four. The power line worker. When a storm knocks out the power, everyone else stays inside. These workers go out.
16. Median pay: about ninety-five thousand. They climb poles and towers next to high-voltage lines, and after a big storm they can work long hours, many days in a row.
## #3 — ELEVATOR MECHANIC
17. Number three. The elevator mechanic. Somebody has to install and fix every elevator you ride. They work inside the shafts, on top of the cars, and in cramped machine rooms.
18. Median pay: almost one hundred ten thousand dollars a year. Many are on call around the clock, because when an elevator gets stuck, someone has to come right now.
## #2 — NUCLEAR REACTOR OPERATOR
19. Number two. The nuclear reactor operator. They sit in the control room of a nuclear power plant and control the reactor itself.
20. Median pay: about one hundred twenty-three thousand. The downside: rotating day and night shifts that wreck your sleep, heavy security, and a job where you must stay fully alert every single minute.
## #1 — AIR TRAFFIC CONTROLLER
21. Number one. The air traffic controller. They guide thousands of planes through the sky every day, and make sure none of them get too close.
22. Median pay: about one hundred forty-eight thousand dollars. That's almost three times the typical American job. But the concentration never stops, and the stress is so high that controllers must retire at fifty-six.
23. And right now there aren't enough of them. Reports put America about three thousand air traffic controllers short, and many work ten-hour days, six days a week.
## WHY NOBODY WANTS THEM
24. So why don't more people take these jobs? Look at the pattern. Heights. Darkness. Danger. Bad smells. Night shifts. Weeks away from home. The pay is high because the price is high.
25. And most of these jobs don't need a university degree. They need training, steady nerves, and a willingness to do what other people won't.
26. So if you're choosing a career, ask two questions, not one. How much does it pay? And what does it cost you? For some people, the trade is worth it.
