## Title
Jobs That Pay Insanely Well (But Nobody Wants Them)

Alternatives: "10 High-Paying Jobs Nobody Wants" · "They Pay $148,000 a Year and Still Can't Find Workers"

## Description
Some jobs pay close to $150,000 a year, and they still can't find enough people. Why? Because of what you have to do to earn it.

Ten jobs that pay surprisingly well, counted down by median pay: the wastewater plant operator, the ironworker, the rig drill operator, the roof bolter, the locomotive engineer, the ship captain, the power line worker, the elevator mechanic, the nuclear reactor operator and the air traffic controller. And the pattern behind them: heights, darkness, danger, night shifts and weeks away from home.

All pay figures are U.S. median annual pay (May 2025). Everyone in this video is a fictional illustration.

Sources:
• U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (median pay, May 2025; all occupations $50,980): water and wastewater treatment plant operators $60,020; structural iron and steel workers $62,780; rotary drill operators (oil and gas) $67,890; locomotive engineers $81,410; captains, mates and pilots of water vessels $92,460; electrical power-line installers and repairers $95,320; elevator and escalator installers and repairers $109,910; nuclear power reactor operators $122,890; air traffic controllers $148,080 (FAA mandatory retirement at 56)
• Roof bolters (mining): BLS OEWS May 2025 as tabulated by the National Mining Association, about $76,000
• Air traffic controller shortage (about 3,000 short; many on 10-hour days, six days a week): NPR, Fox Business, Scripps News (2025–2026), citing FAA and NATCA
