## Title
Why It's So Hard to Be a Construction Worker

## Description
It's 5:15 a.m. It's dark, it's cold, and your knees woke up before you did.

One day as a construction laborer, lived from the inside: the gate at 6:30, the safety meeting that is always about ladders, drywall and concrete carried up stairs because the elevator isn't built yet, a man in a clean white hard hat, the deadliest industry in America, 94 degrees and a concrete pour that won't wait, the pay, the rain days that pay nothing, and a building you can point at six months later.

Based on real research:
• Median pay for construction laborers and helpers: $46,050 a year ($22.14 an hour), below the $49,500 median for all jobs — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2024
• 1,075 construction workers died on the job in 2023, the most of any industry and about 1 in 5 of all U.S. work deaths (5,283) — BLS Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries 2023
• Falls, slips and trips caused 421 of those deaths, most from 6 to 30 feet; ladders and stairs were the main source of 109 — BLS CFOI 2023
• Construction was 6% of the workforce but 36% of occupational heat-related deaths, 1992–2016 — CPWR, American Journal of Industrial Medicine
• The industry needed about 439,000 new workers in 2025 — Associated Builders and Contractors
Load weights, start times and rain-day pay describe common practice, not statistics.
