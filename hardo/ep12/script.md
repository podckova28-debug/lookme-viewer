# Jobs That Will Appear in 10 Years — Episode 12 (~7 min, 27 blocks)

Style: STYLE.md (cook look: thin line, realistic proportions, soft flat colour, light partial setting fading into white). Narrator: free OmniVoice clone of Grady (speed 0.85, plan tempo 1.0).
Format: a countdown, 10 to 1, plus a bonus, like ep11. Each job has its own fictional worker, kept consistent across that job's shots. No real person is drawn or named; no company is named in the voice-over. Everything about the future is phrased as a forecast, proposal or target, not a certainty.

## COLD OPEN
1. In ten years, somebody will have a job that sounds like a joke today. Robot teacher. Heat officer. Deepfake detective. Some of these people are already at work, and almost nobody has heard of them.
2. The World Economic Forum asked more than a thousand employers. Their forecast: about a hundred and seventy million new jobs by 2030, and ninety-two million gone. So here are ten jobs of the next ten years. Forecasts, not promises. But the first job ads are already out.
## #10 ROBOTAXI REMOTE HELPER
3. Number ten. The robotaxi helper. A car with nobody in the driver's seat stops in front of something strange. A fallen tree. A worker waving a flag. It isn't sure what to do. So it asks a human. You.
4. You sit in an office, maybe in another city. You see what the car sees, and you give it advice. You don't steer. The car still drives itself. One robotaxi company has said it keeps about seventy helpers on duty for around three thousand cars.
## #9 DRONE FLEET SUPERVISOR
5. Number nine. The drone fleet supervisor. Today, most drones in America must stay where their pilot can see them. The country's aviation regulator has proposed rules to let them fly much farther, beyond anyone's line of sight.
6. Under that proposal, a drone company needs an operations supervisor and flight coordinators. One person could watch a whole screen of drones, carrying parcels, medicine and cargo, some as heavy as thirteen hundred pounds. You are air traffic control for things with propellers.
## #8 DEEPFAKE DETECTIVE
7. Number eight. The deepfake detective. A video shows a famous face saying something terrible. A voice message from your boss asks for an urgent bank transfer. Is it real? Somebody has to check, frame by frame.
8. Since August 2026, a European Union law says deepfakes must be labeled, and chatbots must tell you they're not human. Labels need checkers. Banks, newsrooms and courts will need people who can look at a face and find the pixel that lies.
## #7 BATTERY RECYCLER
9. Number seven. The battery recycler. Every electric car carries a huge battery under its floor. In the coming years, millions of them will wear out. You open them safely, take them apart, and send the metals back to the factory.
10. The European Union has set targets: half of the lithium in old batteries must be recovered by the end of 2027, and eighty percent by the end of 2031. From 2031, new batteries must contain some recycled metal. Your old car becomes part of a new one.
## #6 CHIEF HEAT OFFICER
11. Number six. The chief heat officer. In 2021, Miami-Dade County in Florida appointed the world's first. The job: protect a city from heat waves, the way a fire chief protects it from fires.
12. You plant shade trees, open cooling centers, paint roofs white, and make sure somebody checks on old people living alone when it's over a hundred degrees. Athens, Freetown in Sierra Leone and Los Angeles have hired heat officers too. More cities will follow.
## #5 DATA CENTER TECHNICIAN
13. Number five. The data center technician. Every chatbot answer, every video and every photo you upload lives in a giant warehouse full of computers. They run all day, they get hot, and they break. You keep them alive.
14. The International Energy Agency expects data centers to use more than twice as much electricity in 2030 as in 2024. That's slightly more than the whole of Japan uses today. Somebody has to cool it, power it, and swap the broken parts at three in the morning.
## #4 CARE ROBOT TECHNICIAN
15. Number four. The care robot technician. Japan is getting older, fast. Its government estimates the country will need about two point seven million care workers in 2040. Today it has fewer than two point two million.
16. So robots are joining the team: lifting aids, walking helpers, sensors that notice when someone falls at night. They need a person to set them up, fix them and teach the staff. Half engineer, half nurse. The robot does the lifting. You do the caring.
## #3 WIND TURBINE TECHNICIAN
17. Number three. The wind turbine technician. According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, it's the fastest-growing job in America, up about sixty percent in ten years. The office is three hundred feet in the air.
18. You climb inside the tower, clip on your harness at the top, and fix a machine the size of a bus while the wind tries to steal your hat. Solar panel installers are close behind, up nearly fifty percent. The future runs on people who aren't afraid of heights.
## #2 AI AUDITOR
19. Number two. The AI auditor. Computer programs already help decide who gets a loan, a job interview, or a place at college. What if the program is unfair? It can't explain itself. So somebody checks it before it goes live.
20. In Europe, AI used for hiring, credit and education counts as high-risk, with strict checks due from December 2027. Auditors test it like detectives: feed it thousands of fake applicants, change one detail, and watch the answer. Same person, different name, different result? Caught.
## #1 ROBOT TRAINER
21. Number one. The robot trainer. Human-shaped robots are learning to walk, carry boxes and fold laundry. They learn by copying people. And somebody has to be the person they copy.
22. In 2024, one American carmaker advertised exactly that: wear a motion-capture suit and a headset, walk for more than seven hours a day, carry up to thirty pounds. Pay: up to forty-eight dollars an hour. There was even a height limit, five foot seven to five foot eleven. You are the robot's role model.
## BONUS: FARMWORKER
23. And a bonus twist. When the World Economic Forum counted which job will add the most workers by 2030, the winner wasn't a coder or an engineer. It was the farmworker. About thirty-five million more.
24. Because the world will still need to eat. The farm will just look different: drones over the fields, sensors in the soil, and a farmer checking the tractor's software update before breakfast.
## WHY
25. So what do these jobs have in common? Each one exists because a new machine arrived, and somebody had to stand next to it. Cars that drive themselves need a helper. Robots need a teacher. Even the smartest AI needs an auditor.
26. And here's the good news. Most of these jobs don't need a genius. They need someone calm, careful and curious, with one new skill. The future isn't only hiring robots. It's hiring the people who look after them.
