## Description

In 1940 a company that built furniture was asked to build a bomber. It had no guns, no turrets and no armour. It was made of balsa and birch plywood by piano makers and cabinet makers. The Air Ministry rejected it twice and called it a folly. By the end of the war the de Havilland Mosquito had the lowest loss rate of any aircraft in RAF Bomber Command, and Hermann Göring said it made him "green and yellow with envy".

This is the story of how the Mosquito was designed, why it was built in secret at a moated country house, how it humiliated the Luftwaffe over Berlin in daylight, and the hidden weakness in its wooden structure that almost ended the programme.

Facts and figures in this video:
- Prototype first flight: 25 November 1940, Hatfield, Geoffrey de Havilland Jr at the controls
- Construction: birch plywood skins over a balsa core, fuselage built in two halves in concrete moulds
- Berlin daylight raid: 30 January 1943, timed to disrupt Göring's and Goebbels' radio speeches
- Operation Jericho: 18 February 1944, Amiens prison, 18 Mosquitos, Group Captain P. C. Pickard killed
- German response: Focke-Wulf Ta 154 "Moskito", cancelled after the Tego-Film glue plant was bombed
- Total built: 7,781 in Britain, Canada and Australia
