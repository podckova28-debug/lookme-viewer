# How Every Norse God Died at Ragnarök — script v1

Format: same as the history channel (cold open → thesis → chapters titled by an object → hook at the end of every chapter →
one subscribe CTA ~3:30 → callback ending). Present tense, third person. Names in narration use simple anglicised spellings
(Ragnarok, Jormungand, Mjolnir, Hodur, Surt) so the TTS reads them cleanly.

=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
Every god in Norse mythology knows how the world will end. They know the day will be called Ragnarok. They know who will kill them. And Odin, the king of the gods, knows exactly which mouth he will die in. He has known for centuries. And still, he cannot stop it. This is how every Norse god died at Ragnarok, and the one mistake that started it all.

=== c01 | The Eye ===
Odin is the oldest and wisest of the gods, and he is obsessed with one thing: knowledge. He gives up one of his eyes to drink from the well of Mimir, the well of wisdom. He hangs himself from the world tree, Yggdrasil, for nine nights, wounded by his own spear, to learn the secret runes. And then he hears a prophecy from a dead seeress. The gods will fall. The sun will go dark. The world will burn and sink into the sea. From that day on, Odin prepares for a battle he already knows he will lose. In his hall, Valhalla, he gathers the bravest warriors who die in battle, so that on the last day the gods will not fight alone. But the end does not begin with a war. It begins with a dream.

=== c02 | The Mistletoe ===
Baldur is the most beloved of all the gods. He is gentle, bright and kind, and one night he dreams of his own death. His mother, Frigg, cannot bear it. She travels through all the worlds and makes every single thing swear never to harm her son. Fire, water, iron, stone, poison, animals and trees. Every thing swears. Except one small plant, the mistletoe, which she thinks is too young to matter. Now Baldur cannot be hurt, and the gods turn it into a game. They throw spears and stones at him and laugh as everything bounces off. But Loki, the trickster, finds out about the mistletoe. He makes a dart from it and puts it in the hands of Hodur, Baldur's blind brother, and guides his aim. The dart flies. And Baldur falls dead. Hel, the ruler of the dead, agrees to let him go, but only if every thing in the world weeps for him. Everything weeps. Except one old giantess in a cave, who refuses. Most likely, it is Loki in disguise. And now the gods know exactly who to blame.

=== c03 | The Chains ===
The gods hunt Loki down. He hides as a salmon in a waterfall, but Thor catches him by the tail. They tie him to three rocks deep under the earth, and they place a venomous snake above his face. Drop by drop, the venom falls. His wife, Sigyn, stays beside him and catches it in a bowl. But when the bowl is full, she has to empty it, and the venom hits his face. Loki screams and twists, and the whole earth shakes. That, the old stories say, is where earthquakes come from. Loki is not the only prisoner. Years before, the gods chained his son, the giant wolf Fenrir, because they feared how big he was growing. Fenrir only agreed to the magic chain if one god put a hand in his mouth as a promise. Only the god Tyr was brave enough. When Fenrir realised he had been tricked, he bit Tyr's hand off. Now the wolf and his father wait in the dark. And both of them are getting free.

=== c04 | The Winter ===
The first sign is the cold. A winter comes that never ends. Snow falls from every direction, the winds cut like knives, and the sun gives no warmth. It is called Fimbulwinter, the great winter. Three winters in a row, with no summer between them. Across the world, families turn on each other. Brothers kill brothers. Then two wolves that have chased the sun and moon across the sky since the beginning of time finally catch them. The sky goes dark. The stars fall. The earth shakes so hard that every chain breaks. And Fenrir is free.

If you like whole stories told in a few minutes, subscribe. Because the army that is now marching on the gods has a ship made from the fingernails of the dead.

=== c05 | The Horn ===
From the frozen north comes Naglfar, a ship built from the uncut fingernails of the dead, carrying an army of giants. The sea rises and floods the land, because the World Serpent, Jormungand, a snake so huge it circles the whole earth and bites its own tail, is rising out of the ocean. Loki breaks his chains and leads the dead out of the underworld. Fenrir runs with his lower jaw scraping the ground and his upper jaw touching the sky, and he would open it wider if there were room. And from the south comes Surt, a fire giant with a flaming sword, leading the sons of fire. They ride over Bifrost, the rainbow bridge to the home of the gods, and the bridge breaks under them. Heimdall, the watchman of the gods, who can hear grass growing, lifts his great horn, the Gjallarhorn, and blows it so loud that every world hears. The gods arm themselves. Odin rides one last time to the well of Mimir for advice. Then they march out to a plain called Vigrid, a hundred miles wide in every direction. And the first duel begins.

=== c06 | The Wolf ===
Odin rides first, in a golden helmet, with his spear Gungnir in his hand. He goes straight for Fenrir. This is the moment he has seen in the prophecy for his whole life. He fights. And Fenrir swallows him whole. The king of the gods, the one who gave an eye for wisdom, dies inside the mouth of the wolf. His wife Frigg loses her husband, after already losing her son. But Odin has one more son. Vidar, the silent god, steps up to the wolf. On one foot he wears a shoe made from all the leather scraps that people have ever cut from their shoes. He plants that foot on Fenrir's lower jaw, grabs the upper jaw with his hands, and tears the wolf apart. Odin is avenged. But on the other side of the field, his strongest son is facing a snake.

=== c07 | The Serpent ===
Thor and the World Serpent have hated each other forever. Once, Thor went fishing with an ox head as bait, hooked the serpent and nearly pulled it out of the sea, before a terrified giant cut the line. Now there is no line to cut. Thor swings his hammer, Mjolnir, and crushes the serpent's head. Jormungand is dead. Thor has won. He turns around and takes one step. Then another. And another. Nine steps. Then he falls dead, poisoned by the venom the serpent sprayed over him in the fight. And the god of fertility is about to pay for a love story.

=== c08 | The Antler ===
Freyr is the god of sun, rain and harvest. He once owned a magic sword that could fight on its own. But he fell in love with a giantess named Gerd, and to win her, he gave that sword away to his servant. Now he faces Surt, the fire giant, with nothing in his hand but the antler of a deer. He fights bravely. But an antler is not a sword. Freyr falls. The last guardians are left, and each of them is about to meet their perfect enemy.

=== c09 | The Hound ===
Tyr, the god who lost his hand to Fenrir, fights Garm, the monstrous hound that guards the gates of the dead. They kill each other. And Heimdall, the watchman, finally faces Loki, the god who started all of this with a dart of mistletoe. They have been enemies for ages. They fight until both of them are dead. Almost every great god is gone now. Odin. Thor. Freyr. Tyr. Heimdall. Loki. Only one figure is still standing in the middle of the field.

=== c10 | The Fire ===
Surt raises his flaming sword and throws fire across the whole world. The flames climb up the trunk of the world tree. They burn the halls of the gods, the homes of men and the lands of the giants. The heat reaches the sky. And then, slowly, the burning earth sinks into the sea. Darkness. Silence. Ragnarok is over. But the prophecy that Odin heard had one more line.

=== c11 | The Return ===
Out of the water, the earth rises again, green and new. Fields grow without being sown. A daughter of the old sun, as bright as her mother, begins to shine. A few gods have survived. Vidar, who killed the wolf. His brother Vali. Thor's sons, Modi and Magni, who now carry their father's hammer. And from the land of the dead, Baldur comes back, together with Hodur, the blind brother who killed him. In the grass, they find the golden game pieces the old gods used to play with. And two humans, a woman named Lif and a man named Lifthrasir, who hid inside the world tree through the whole fire, walk out into the morning to start the human race again. The gods knew how they would die. They fought anyway. And that is why, a thousand years later, we still remember their names.
