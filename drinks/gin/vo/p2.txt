In sixteen eighty eight, a Dutch prince, William of Orange, became King of England.
He was at war with France.
So he did two things.
He put heavy taxes on French brandy.
And he made it legal for almost anyone to distil spirits from English grain, with no licence at all.
Farmers loved it, because it gave them a market for bad barley.
Landowners loved it.
Parliament loved it.
Within a few years, there were stills in kitchens, sheds and back rooms all over London.
The English couldn't say jeneever, so they shortened it.
Gin.
Cheaper than beer.
Stronger than wine.
And sold by anyone, to anyone.
What could possibly go wrong?
