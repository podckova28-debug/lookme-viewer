By the seventeen twenties, London was in the grip of what we now call the Gin Craze.
Remember the coffeehouses from our coffee episode?
The penny universities, where merchants talked business and scientists talked ideas?
A few streets away, in the poorer parts of town, a penny bought something very different.
There were thousands of gin shops in London.
Gin was sold from barrows, from cellars, from barber shops and prisons.
Some of it was flavoured with turpentine.
Some was strengthened with sulphuric acid.
Workers were paid in gin.
Mothers gave it to crying babies to keep them quiet.
People called it Mother's Ruin.
In some years, more people were buried in London than were born there.
And then came a crime that shocked the whole city.
