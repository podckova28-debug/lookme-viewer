By the early fifteen hundreds, coffee had spread to Mecca, and Cairo.
And a new kind of place appeared.
The coffeehouse.
Men came to drink, play chess, listen to music, and above all, talk.
About poetry.
About prices.
About the people in charge.
And that was the problem.
In fifteen eleven, the governor of Mecca, Kyre Beg, banned coffee.
Sacks of beans were burned in the streets.
But the Sultan in Cairo loved coffee, and the ban was soon overturned.
In fifteen fifty five, two men from Syria opened the first coffeehouse in Istanbul.
People called these places schools of the wise.
Rulers called them something else.
And for the next hundred years, sultans banned them, and opened them, and banned them again.
Coffee was too popular to stop.
So the next question was, who would get rich from it?
