In sixteen seventy four, a pamphlet appeared in London.
The Women's Petition Against Coffee.
It complained that men spent all day in coffeehouses, and came home useless.
The next year, King Charles the Second went further.
He banned coffeehouses completely.
He was afraid they were places where people plotted against him.
The ban lasted eleven days.
The public was furious, and the king backed down.
In Paris, the Caffay Pro-cop opened in sixteen eighty six.
The writer Voltaire is said to have drunk forty or fifty cups a day.
In Leipzig, the composer Bach even wrote a funny little opera about a girl who refuses to give up coffee.
And in Prussia, King Frederick the Great told his people to drink beer instead.
He even hired former soldiers to sniff the streets for the smell of illegal roasting.
They were called the coffee sniffers.
Europe was hooked.
But someone had to grow all that coffee.
