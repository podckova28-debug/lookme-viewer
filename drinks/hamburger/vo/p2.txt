By the eighteen eighties, Hamburg steak was on menus all over New York.
It was cheap.
Grinding made tough, old beef easy to chew.
Great news, if you had bad teeth.
Slightly worrying news, if you wondered what was in it.
Then came hand cranked meat grinders, cheap enough for every butcher.
Suddenly, minced beef was everywhere.
But a steak on a plate has one big problem.
You need a table.
And America was in a hurry.
Workers ate standing up.
People at fairs ate while walking.
Somebody had to put that steak into bread.
The question is, who?
