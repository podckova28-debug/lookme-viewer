French kings are crowned in the cathedral of Rance, but Rance is deep in enemy land.
The generals want to go slowly.
Joan wants to go now.
In June, the French retake the towns along the Lwahr, one after another.
On the eighteenth of June, at Pah-tay, they catch the English army in the open and destroy it.
The great English commander, John Talbot, is taken prisoner.
Then the army marches north, and town after town opens its gates without a fight.
On the seventeenth of July, fourteen twenty nine, in Rance cathedral, the Doh-fan is crowned King Charles the Seventh.
Joan stands near the altar with her banner.
Asked later why her banner was there, she answers: it had shared the hardship, so it was right that it should share the honour.
In less than three months, a peasant girl has turned the war around.
Now she wants Paris.
