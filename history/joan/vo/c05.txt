Joan has no rank and no command.
The captains hold their councils without her.
So she simply rides out, banner in hand, wherever the fighting is, and the soldiers follow her.
On the fourth of May, the French take a fortress east of the city.
On the sixth, another one.
On the seventh, they attack the Too-rell, the stone towers that guard the bridge.
Around midday, an arrow strikes Joan between her neck and her shoulder.
She is carried away from the walls, and the arrow is pulled out.
In the evening, she comes back with her banner.
The soldiers see it, and charge again.
The Too-rell fall.
The next morning, the eighth of May, the English army lines up outside the city, then turns around and marches away.
A siege of seven months has been broken in nine days.
Joan is seventeen.
From now on, they call her the Maid of Or-lay-on.
And she has only one word in mind: Rance.
