But the new king prefers talking to fighting.
He signs truces with Burgundy, and the army waits for weeks.
On the eighth of September, Joan finally attacks Paris, at the gate of Saint Ono-ray.
She is standing at the edge of the moat, testing the water with her lance, when a crossbow bolt hits her in the thigh.
She refuses to leave until dark.
The next day, the king orders a retreat, and soon the army is sent home.
In December, Charles makes Joan and her whole family nobles, as a reward, and perhaps as a way to keep her quiet.
She spends a restless winter at court.
And in the spring, her voices tell her something new.
Before midsummer, she will be captured.
