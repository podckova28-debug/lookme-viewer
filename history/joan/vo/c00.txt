Roo-on, Normandy.
The thirtieth of May, fourteen thirty one.
A crowd fills the old market square.
In the middle stands a high scaffold, and on it, a nineteen-year-old girl in a long dress and a paper cap with four words written on it: heretic, relapsed, apostate, idolater.
She asks for a cross.
An English soldier takes two pieces of wood and ties them together for her.
Two years earlier, this girl led the army of France, broke a siege that had lasted seven months, and put a crown on the head of a king.
Today, that king does nothing at all.
This is the entire life of Joan of Arc, the peasant girl who turned a war around in a single year, and the people who could not forgive her for it.
