She is born around fourteen twelve in Dom-ray-mee, a small village on the river Muhz, on the eastern edge of France.
Her father, Zhahk, is a farmer.
Her mother, Izabel, teaches her to pray and to spin wool, but not to read.
France is losing a war that has already lasted more than seventy years.
The English king holds Paris and the north, together with his allies, the Burgundians.
In fourteen twenty, a treaty even disinherits the French heir, the Doh-fan Charles, and promises his crown to England.
Soldiers raid the villages along the Muhz, and once, Joan's family has to flee their own home.
Then, at about thirteen, in her father's garden at midday, Joan sees a light and hears a voice.
Later she will say it was the archangel Michael, and after him Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret.
For years she tells no one.
Because the voices are giving her an order no peasant girl could obey: go into France, and save it.
