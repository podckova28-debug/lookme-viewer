On the twenty fourth of May, in the cemetery of Saint Oo-wen, Joan is placed on a platform in front of a crowd.
A preacher denounces her.
The executioner waits with his cart.
Terrified, she agrees to sign a short paper, admitting that she was wrong, and promising to wear a woman's dress.
She makes her mark at the bottom.
Her sentence becomes prison for life.
But four days later, she is found in her cell in men's clothes again.
Why is still debated.
Some say her guards took her dress away.
Some say she wore men's clothes to protect herself from them.
Joan herself says that her voices were sad that she had given in to save her life.
For the court, it is enough.
She is now a relapsed heretic.
And for a relapsed heretic, there is only one sentence.
