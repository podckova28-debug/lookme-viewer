In May fourteen twenty eight, at sixteen, she walks to the fortress town of Voh-koo-lur and asks its captain, Robert de Bo-dree-koor, for an escort to the Doh-fan.
He laughs, and tells her cousin to take her home to her father and box her ears.
She comes back in January.
By now the town has heard an old prophecy: France will be lost by a woman, and saved by a maiden.
People start to follow Joan in the street.
In February, news arrives of another French defeat, and Bo-dree-koor finally gives in.
Joan cuts her hair short, puts on men's clothes, and rides out with six men.
For eleven days they travel, mostly at night, through land held by the enemy, nearly three hundred and fifty miles.
At the end of the road is a castle on the river Lwahr, and a king who has never been crowned.
