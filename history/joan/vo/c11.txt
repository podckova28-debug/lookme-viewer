On the morning of the thirtieth of May, fourteen thirty one, she is taken to the old market square.
A priest brings a crucifix from a nearby church and holds it up in front of her, so that she can see it until the end.
Her last word is the name of Jesus.
Afterwards, her ashes are thrown into the river Sen, so that nothing of her will remain.
According to one witness, an English official walks away saying: we are lost, we have burned a holy person.
Twenty two years later, the English are driven out of almost all of France.
In fourteen fifty six, a new church court, ordered by the Pope, declares her trial null and void.
In nineteen twenty, the Catholic Church makes her a saint.
The girl who asked for two pieces of wood tied into a cross was burned as a heretic at nineteen.
Four hundred and eighty nine years later, the same Church called her a saint.
