On the twenty third of May, fourteen thirty, she leads a small attack out of the town of Kom-pyen, which the Burgundians are besieging.
The attack fails.
As the French fall back, the gate of the town is closed, and Joan is left outside.
An archer grabs her by her cloak and pulls her off her horse.
She is now a prisoner of the Burgundians.
King Charles, who owes her his crown, never pays a ransom.
She is held in the castle of Bo-rev-wahr, and one night, she jumps from the top of the tower, about twenty metres down.
She survives, badly hurt.
In November, the Burgundians sell her to the English for ten thousand pounds.
The English do not simply want her dead.
They want a church court to prove that her voices came from the devil, and so did the crown of the king she made.
