# The Entire Life of NAPOLEON — pilot in the Food & Drinks format (ketchup formula)

Format test for the personalities channel, copied from drinks/ketchup: dry humour in every part, second-person "you" scenes,
cartoon gag frames, white background ink + watercolour, handwritten notes with dates and numbers, a new picture every 2–3 s,
one sentence per line (line index = shot cue). Running gag: James Gillray, the British cartoonist who keeps drawing him tiny
("Little Boney"); payoff = the real height. Pilot = p0 (hook) + p1 (Corsica, school). Numbers spelled out for TTS.

=== p0 ===
You are a British sailor.
It's October, eighteen fifteen, and your ship has just anchored off a tiny volcanic rock in the middle of the South Atlantic.
Saint Helena.
Almost two thousand kilometres from the nearest coast.
Your only important passenger is a pale, slightly chubby man in a plain green coat.
Ten years ago, he ruled most of Europe.
An emperor gave him his own daughter as a wife.
Seven alliances of countries were formed, one after another, just to stop him.
And now his entire empire is a damp farmhouse.
With rats.
You know one thing about him.
He was short.
Except he wasn't.
He was about one metre sixty nine, completely average for a Frenchman of his time.
That joke was invented by a British cartoonist who simply refused to draw him any bigger.
So how did a poor boy from an island that wasn't even French end up ruling Europe?
And how did he end up here, with the rats?
Spoiler.
It involves a lot of cannons, one very bad winter, and that cartoonist.
Who is not done yet.

=== p1 ===
The story starts on a different island.
Corsica, in the Mediterranean.
For centuries, it belonged to the Republic of Genoa.
In seventeen sixty eight, Genoa handed it over to France.
One year later, on the fifteenth of August, seventeen sixty nine, a baby is born in the town of Ajaccio.
His name is Napoleone di Buonaparte.
Not exactly a French name.
His father, Carlo, is a lawyer with a noble title and almost no money.
His mother, Letizia, will have thirteen children, and eight of them survive.
At nine, Napoleone is shipped off to a military school in France, at Brienne.
He barely speaks French.
The other boys mock his accent, his name, and his clothes.
He has almost no friends.
So he reads.
History, maths, Alexander the Great, Julius Caesar.
At fifteen, he gets into the top military school in Paris.
Then his father dies, and the teenager takes charge of a family with seven brothers and sisters and no money.
He finishes a two year course in one year.
At sixteen, he's an artillery officer.
Ranked forty second out of fifty eight.
Not exactly a genius.
Yet.
Four years later, France explodes.
And explosions are exactly what an artillery officer is good at.

=== p2 ===
In seventeen eighty nine, the French Revolution begins.
The king loses his power, and a few years later, his head.
Napoleone goes home to Corsica and tries to join the local revolution.
It goes badly.
In seventeen ninety three, the Buonaparte family has to flee the island, with almost nothing.
They land in France as refugees.
Napoleone stops being Napoleone.
From now on, he signs his name the French way.
Napoleon Bonaparte.
That same year, the British capture the French naval port of Toulon.
Bonaparte is a twenty four year old captain, but he sees what the generals don't.
Put the cannons on the hills above the harbour, and the British fleet has to leave.
It works.
At twenty four, he is promoted to brigadier general.
A year later, his political friends fall from power, and he is thrown in prison.
Two weeks, and he's out.
In October seventeen ninety five, a royalist mob marches on the government in Paris.
Bonaparte meets them with cannons loaded with grapeshot.
The mob is gone in minutes.
The government is very grateful.
And Paris society wants to meet the young general with the cannons.

=== p3 ===
At a party in Paris, he meets a widow named Rose de Beauharnais.
She is elegant, charming, six years older, and deeply in debt.
He falls madly in love.
He doesn't like the name Rose, so he simply calls her Joséphine.
They marry in March, seventeen ninety six.
Two days later, he leaves to command the French army in Italy.
It's a hungry, ragged army with no boots and no pay.
Within a year, it beats the Austrians again and again.
Lodi.
The bridge at Arcole.
Rivoli.
He sends home gold, paintings and statues.
And he sends Joséphine love letters, sometimes several a day.
She barely answers.
She's busy with a handsome young officer named Hippolyte Charles.
Meanwhile in London, a cartoonist named James Gillray has noticed the French general.
He gives him a name.
Little Boney.
And he starts drawing him smaller, and smaller, and smaller.

=== p4 ===
In seventeen ninety eight, Bonaparte sails to Egypt, to cut off Britain's road to India.
He brings an army.
And more than a hundred and fifty scientists.
Near the pyramids, he defeats the Mamluk cavalry.
Then, one week later, the British admiral Nelson destroys his fleet at the Battle of the Nile.
Bonaparte and his army are now stuck in Egypt.
His scientists measure everything, draw everything, and take everything home that isn't nailed down.
His soldiers find a stone near the town of Rosetta, with the same text in three scripts.
The Rosetta Stone will later unlock Egyptian hieroglyphs.
It ends up in the British Museum, where it still is today.
Then news arrives from Paris.
The government is weak, unpopular, and losing the war in Europe.
So Bonaparte does something bold.
He leaves his army in Egypt, and sails home.
In November seventeen ninety nine, he and his allies take over the government.
Not everything goes to plan.
When he speaks to the parliament, the deputies shout at him and shove him, and he has to be rescued by his soldiers.
But by the end of the day, he is First Consul of France.
He's thirty years old.

=== p5 ===
And he gets to work.
He creates the Bank of France.
New schools.
New roads.
And a single set of laws for the whole country, the Civil Code.
Many countries still use laws based on it today.
In eighteen oh four, he decides First Consul is not enough.
On the second of December, in Notre Dame cathedral, the pope himself comes to crown him.
Bonaparte takes the crown from the altar and puts it on his own head.
Then he crowns Joséphine.
The painting of that day is ten metres wide, and his mother is in it.
She actually stayed at home.
In eighteen oh five, at Trafalgar, Nelson destroys the French navy for good.
Napoleon will never invade Britain.
But six weeks later, at Austerlitz, he crushes the armies of Austria and Russia in a single day.
Gillray draws him carving up the globe like a Christmas pudding.
Still tiny, of course.
By eighteen ten, he rules or controls most of Europe.
There's just one problem.
He has no son.

=== p6 ===
Joséphine can't give him a child.
So in eighteen ten, he divorces the woman he once wrote ten letters a day to.
He marries Marie Louise, the eighteen year old daughter of the Austrian emperor.
The same emperor he had beaten twice.
A year later, he finally has a son.
He makes the baby King of Rome.
Then he makes the biggest mistake of his life.
In June eighteen twelve, he invades Russia with roughly six hundred thousand men.
The Russians don't fight.
They retreat, and burn everything behind them.
At Borodino, both armies lose tens of thousands of men in a single day.
A week later, Napoleon rides into Moscow.
It's almost empty.
And that night, it starts to burn.
He waits for the Tsar to surrender.
The Tsar doesn't answer.
So in October, the Grande Armée starts the long walk home.
Then the winter comes.
Snow, hunger, cold, and Cossacks.
Fewer than one in five of his soldiers make it back.

=== p7 ===
Now everyone attacks at once.
In eighteen thirteen, at Leipzig, more than half a million soldiers fight for three days.
Napoleon loses.
In April eighteen fourteen, Paris falls, and he gives up the throne.
The allies send him to Elba, a small island off Italy.
And, to be polite, they let him keep the title of emperor.
Emperor of Elba.
Twelve thousand people, and a lot of goats.
He lasts ten months.
In February eighteen fifteen, he escapes with about a thousand men, and lands in the south of France.
The king sends an army to stop him.
Napoleon walks up to the soldiers, opens his coat, and says: if any of you wants to kill your emperor, here I am.
They switch sides.
On the twentieth of March, he is back in Paris, and the king has already fled.
Europe sends everything it has.
On the eighteenth of June, eighteen fifteen, near a Belgian village called Waterloo, he meets the British under Wellington.
By evening, the Prussians arrive.
And it's over.
He surrenders to the captain of a British warship.
Which brings us back to a tiny rock in the South Atlantic.
And to you.

=== p8 ===
On Saint Helena, Napoleon dictates his memoirs, plays cards, and gardens.
He fights constantly with the British governor, Hudson Lowe, about everything from money to the name on his letters.
The British call him General Bonaparte.
He refuses to open anything that isn't addressed to the Emperor.
On the fifth of May, eighteen twenty one, he dies.
He's fifty one.
The doctors find stomach cancer, the same illness that killed his father.
Years later, people find arsenic in his hair, and in the green wallpaper of his room.
Historians still argue about it.
His doctor measures the body.
Five feet two inches.
In French units.
In English inches, that's about five foot six.
Completely average.
But the British read five foot two, and the legend was born.
As for James Gillray, he died in June eighteen fifteen, the same month as Waterloo.
He never drew the end of the story.
He didn't need to.
His tiny Napoleon outlived them both.
In eighteen forty, France brings Napoleon home, and buries him in Paris, under a golden dome, in a tomb so big you have to look down at it.
Which might be the best joke of all.
If you liked this story, subscribe, there's a new life every week.
So, hero or tyrant?
Tell me in the comments.

## Facts to keep straight
- HMS Northumberland anchored at St Helena 15 Oct 1815, he landed 17 Oct; Longwood House was damp and rat-infested (Las Cases, O'Meara).
- St Helena ≈ 1,900–2,000 km from the African coast. Seven Coalitions (1792–1815). Marie-Louise, daughter of Emperor Francis I of Austria (1810).
- Height: 5 pieds 2 pouces (French) ≈ 1.68–1.69 m; "short" myth amplified by James Gillray's "Little Boney" caricatures.
- Corsica: Treaty of Versailles 1768 (Genoa → France). Born Ajaccio 15 Aug 1769. Carlo Buonaparte (lawyer, minor nobility), Letizia Ramolino: 13 children, 8 survived.
- Brienne 1779 (age 9, after months at Autun learning French), mocked for accent; École Militaire Paris Oct 1784 (age 15); father died Feb 1785;
  graduated Sept 1785 in one year, artillery second lieutenant, ranked 42nd of 58.

- 1789 Revolution; Louis XVI executed Jan 1793. Paoli split, family fled Corsica June 1793. Signs "Bonaparte" from 1796 (simplified in script: "from now on").
- Toulon siege Sep–Dec 1793, captain → brigadier general 22 Dec 1793 (age 24). Arrested Aug 1794 after Thermidor, ~2 weeks. 13 Vendémiaire (5 Oct 1795) "whiff of grapeshot".
- Joséphine (Rose) de Beauharnais, b. 1763 (6 yrs older), married 9 Mar 1796, left for Italy 11 Mar. Lodi May 1796, Arcole Nov 1796, Rivoli Jan 1797. Hippolyte Charles affair. Gillray "Little Boney" from c.1803 (script places Gillray noticing him from Italy era: Gillray caricatured him from 1797/98).
- Egypt 1798, 167 savants; Battle of the Pyramids 21 Jul 1798; Nile 1–3 Aug 1798; Rosetta Stone Jul 1799, British Museum since 1802. Left Egypt Aug 1799. 18–19 Brumaire (9–10 Nov 1799), jostled in the Council of Five Hundred. First Consul at 30.
- Banque de France 1800, lycées 1802, Civil Code 1804. Coronation 2 Dec 1804, Pius VII present, crowned himself. David's painting ~10 m wide, Letizia painted in though absent.
- Trafalgar 21 Oct 1805; Austerlitz 2 Dec 1805. Gillray "The Plumb-pudding in danger" (Feb 1805).
- Divorce Dec 1809/Jan 1810; Marie-Louise (b. Dec 1791, 18) married Apr 1810; Austria beaten 1805 and 1809. Son 20 Mar 1811, King of Rome.
- Russia: crossed Niemen 24 Jun 1812, ~600,000 (incl. later reinforcements); Borodino 7 Sep (~70,000 casualties total); Moscow 14 Sep, fire; retreat 19 Oct; returned fewer than 100,000 (estimates vary).
- Leipzig 16–19 Oct 1813 (~500,000+). Abdication 6 Apr 1814; Elba pop. ~12,000. Escape 26 Feb 1815, landed 1 Mar with ~1,000; Laffrey 7 Mar ("S'il est parmi vous un soldat qui veuille tuer son Empereur, me voilà"); Paris 20 Mar. Waterloo 18 Jun 1815; surrendered to Capt. Maitland, HMS Bellerophon, 15 Jul.
- St Helena: Hudson Lowe, "General Bonaparte" dispute. Died 5 May 1821 aged 51; autopsy: stomach cancer (father Carlo died of stomach cancer 1785). Arsenic in hair (1960s studies), Scheele's green wallpaper theory. Antommarchi: 5 pieds 2 pouces ≈ 1.68–1.69 m ≈ 5 ft 6 in. Gillray died 1 Jun 1815. Retour des cendres 1840, Invalides dome; the sarcophagus sits in an open crypt viewed from above.
