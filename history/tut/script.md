=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
The Valley of the Kings, Egypt. The twenty sixth of November, nineteen twenty two. An English archaeologist named Howard Carter makes a small hole in an ancient plastered doorway, and holds a candle up to it. Hot air from inside makes the flame flicker. Behind him, the man paying for the dig asks: can you see anything? Carter is silent for a long time. Then he says: yes, wonderful things. For three thousand years, the king who was buried here was one of the least important rulers Egypt ever had. Today, he is the most famous. This is the entire life of Tutankhamun, the boy king who did almost nothing, and the reason the whole world knows his name.

=== c01 | The Sun Disk ===
He is born around thirteen forty one BC, in a brand new city in the desert called Akhetaten, today known as Amarna. His name is Tutankhaten, the living image of the Aten. His father, according to DNA studies, is almost certainly the pharaoh Akhenaten, the strangest king in Egyptian history. Akhenaten has done something no pharaoh dared before. He has abandoned the old gods of Egypt, Amun, Osiris and hundreds of others, and ordered the country to worship just one: the Aten, the disk of the sun. He closed temples, sent away their priests, and moved the capital to this new city. The boy grows up among sun-filled courtyards and images of his family under the rays of the sun. His mother, the same DNA studies suggest, was his father's own sister. But when Tutankhaten is still a small child, his father dies. And within a few years, the boy is the only male heir left.

=== c02 | The Crook ===
Around thirteen thirty two BC, at about eight or nine years old, Tutankhaten becomes pharaoh of Egypt. He is handed the crook and the flail, the symbols of a king, and a wife: his half-sister, Ankhesenpaaten, a daughter of Akhenaten and the famous queen Nefertiti. She is a few years older than him. A child cannot rule an empire. Real power sits with two older men. Ay, a veteran courtier who served his father, and Horemheb, the commander of the army. And they have a plan for the young king. Because Egypt is angry. The old priests have lost their temples, the people have lost their gods, and the country blames the family of Akhenaten. To survive, the boy will have to give up the one thing his father believed in.

=== c03 | The Stela ===
In the first years of his reign, the king changes his name. Tutankhaten, the living image of the Aten, becomes Tutankhamun, the living image of Amun, the old god his father had rejected. His wife becomes Ankhesenamun. The court leaves the city of the sun, and Amarna is slowly abandoned to the sand. At the great temple of Karnak, the young king sets up a large stone slab, the Restoration Stela. On it, he describes a country in ruins, where the gods had turned their backs on Egypt, and promises to rebuild their temples, and to make new statues of gold. Most historians believe that the words were written by his advisers, not by a nine-year-old boy. Either way, it works. The old gods are back. And the king's own father is quietly becoming a name no one wants to say.

=== c04 | The Cane ===
Statues and paintings show Tutankhamun as a perfect young god, hunting lions and riding into battle. The real king may have looked quite different. Modern scans of his mummy suggest a damaged left foot, with signs of bone disease, and DNA tests found traces of malaria. In his tomb, archaeologists will find around one hundred and thirty walking sticks, some of them clearly used. Many experts think he walked with a cane for much of his life. He and Ankhesenamun have no surviving children. In the tomb lie two tiny coffins, holding two daughters who were born dead. The young couple are the last of their family line. And the boy king is now almost a man, perhaps ready to rule on his own.

=== c05 | The Chariot ===
Around thirteen twenty three BC, in about the ninth or tenth year of his reign, Tutankhamun dies, at roughly nineteen. There is no record of how. For decades, an old X-ray showing a bone fragment inside his skull convinced many people that he was murdered by a blow to the head. Modern CT scans, in two thousand five, suggested something else. The fragment probably came loose during mummification, and the king had a badly broken left leg, broken shortly before his death. Some researchers think he fell from a chariot. Others think a broken leg, together with malaria and a weak body, was enough to kill him. The debate is still open. What is certain is what happens next. Egypt has no pharaoh, and no heir. And the young widow, Ankhesenamun, does something astonishing.

=== c06 | The Letter ===
According to records found in the archives of the Hittites, Egypt's great rival in the north, an Egyptian queen, recently widowed, sends a letter to the Hittite king, Suppiluliuma. My husband has died, she writes. I have no son. They say you have many sons. Give me one of your sons, and he will become my husband. I will never take a servant of mine and make him my husband. The Hittite king cannot believe it. He sends an envoy to check, and the queen writes again. Finally, he sends a prince, named Zannanza. On the way to Egypt, the prince is killed. No one knows by whom. Most historians think the widow was Ankhesenamun. And the servant she refused to marry may have been the old courtier, Ay. Because Ay is the man who now takes the throne.

=== c07 | The Tomb ===
In Egypt, the new king must bury the old one. And Tutankhamun's burial seems rushed. His tomb in the Valley of the Kings is small, only four rooms, and may have been built for someone else. One of the paintings on the wall shows Ay himself, wearing the leopard skin of a priest, performing the opening of the mouth ritual for the dead king, a duty normally done by his heir. Into the four little rooms, the workers squeeze thousands of objects. Chariots, beds, thrones, weapons, jewellery, food, games, his childhood chair, even a lock of his grandmother's hair. Then the tomb is sealed. Within a few years, it is robbed at least twice, and sealed again. And then, the people who come after him decide that he should never have existed.

=== c08 | The Chisel ===
Ay rules for only about four years. After him, the army commander Horemheb becomes pharaoh, and he sets out to erase the entire Amarna family from history. His workers break up the temples of the sun god, and use the stones as filling for new buildings. The names of Akhenaten, of Ay, and of Tutankhamun are cut out of monuments. Later lists of kings jump straight from the pharaoh before Akhenaten to Horemheb, as if the boy king had never ruled at all. About two hundred years later, workmen cutting a tomb for another pharaoh, Ramesses the Sixth, build their stone huts right on top of the entrance to Tutankhamun's tomb. Their rubble covers the steps completely. The king's enemies wanted him forgotten. They succeed so well that the tomb robbers forget him too. For more than three thousand years.

=== c09 | The Step ===
In the early nineteen hundreds, many archaeologists believe the Valley of the Kings is empty. Howard Carter disagrees. He has found small objects with the name of a little-known king, Tutankhamun, and believes his tomb is still there. For five seasons, paid by an English aristocrat, Lord Carnarvon, he digs and finds almost nothing. In nineteen twenty two, Carnarvon tells him this is the last season. On the fourth of November, under the remains of the old workmen's huts, his team uncovers a stone step. Then another, and another. Sixteen steps lead down to a sealed doorway. Carter covers it up again and sends a telegram to England. Three weeks later, Carnarvon arrives, and Carter makes a hole in the second doorway, and holds up his candle. And what he sees changes the history of archaeology.

=== c10 | The Mask ===
The tomb is almost intact. It takes Carter ten years to empty it, and he records more than five thousand objects. In the burial chamber, there are four golden shrines, one inside the other, like Russian dolls. Inside them, a stone sarcophagus. Inside that, three coffins. The innermost coffin is made of solid gold and weighs about one hundred and ten kilograms. And on the head of the king lies a mask of gold, lapis lazuli and coloured glass, weighing about ten kilograms. It shows the face of a calm young man, with large eyes, staring straight ahead. Newspapers all over the world print photographs of the treasure. Fashion, jewellery and cinemas copy the Egyptian style. Tutankhamun, the forgotten king, becomes the most famous pharaoh in history. And then, in April nineteen twenty three, the man who paid for all of it suddenly dies.

=== c11 | The Mosquito ===
Lord Carnarvon is bitten by a mosquito in Egypt. While shaving, he cuts the bite open, it becomes infected, and in April nineteen twenty three he dies in Cairo of blood poisoning and pneumonia. Newspapers have been looking for a story, and they find one. The curse of the pharaoh. Legend says the lights of Cairo went out at the moment of his death, and his dog died at the same moment in England. Every later death connected to the tomb is blamed on the curse. But Howard Carter, the man who opened the tomb, lives for another sixteen years, and dies in London in nineteen thirty nine, aged sixty four. Today, the king's mask and treasures fill the halls of a new museum near the pyramids of Giza, visited by millions. His ancient enemies chiselled his name off every wall, so that no one would remember him. Three thousand years later, his is the one name everyone remembers.
