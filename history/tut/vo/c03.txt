In the first years of his reign, the king changes his name.
Too-tan-kah-ten, the living image of the Ah-ten, becomes Too-tan-kah-moon, the living image of Ah-moon, the old god his father had rejected.
His wife becomes Ahnk-eh-sen-ah-moon.
The court leaves the city of the sun, and Ah-mar-nah is slowly abandoned to the sand.
At the great temple of Karnak, the young king sets up a large stone slab, the Restoration Stela.
On it, he describes a country in ruins, where the gods had turned their backs on Egypt, and promises to rebuild their temples, and to make new statues of gold.
Most historians believe that the words were written by his advisers, not by a nine-year-old boy.
Either way, it works.
The old gods are back.
And the king's own father is quietly becoming a name no one wants to say.
