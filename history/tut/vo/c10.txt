The tomb is almost intact.
It takes Carter ten years to empty it, and he records more than five thousand objects.
In the burial chamber, there are four golden shrines, one inside the other, like Russian dolls.
Inside them, a stone sarcophagus.
Inside that, three coffins.
The innermost coffin is made of solid gold and weighs about one hundred and ten kilograms.
And on the head of the king lies a mask of gold, lapis lazuli and coloured glass, weighing about ten kilograms.
It shows the face of a calm young man, with large eyes, staring straight ahead.
Newspapers all over the world print photographs of the treasure.
Fashion, jewellery and cinemas copy the Egyptian style.
Too-tan-kah-moon, the forgotten king, becomes the most famous pharaoh in history.
And then, in April nineteen twenty three, the man who paid for all of it suddenly dies.
