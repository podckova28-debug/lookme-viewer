In Egypt, the new king must bury the old one.
And Too-tan-kah-moon's burial seems rushed.
His tomb in the Valley of the Kings is small, only four rooms, and may have been built for someone else.
One of the paintings on the wall shows Eye himself, wearing the leopard skin of a priest, performing the opening of the mouth ritual for the dead king, a duty normally done by his heir.
Into the four little rooms, the workers squeeze thousands of objects.
Chariots, beds, thrones, weapons, jewellery, food, games, his childhood chair, even a lock of his grandmother's hair.
Then the tomb is sealed.
Within a few years, it is robbed at least twice, and sealed again.
And then, the people who come after him decide that he should never have existed.
