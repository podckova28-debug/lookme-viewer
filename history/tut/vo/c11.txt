Lord Car-nar-von is bitten by a mosquito in Egypt.
While shaving, he cuts the bite open, it becomes infected, and in April nineteen twenty three he dies in Cairo of blood poisoning and pneumonia.
Newspapers have been looking for a story, and they find one.
The curse of the pharaoh.
Legend says the lights of Cairo went out at the moment of his death, and his dog died at the same moment in England.
Every later death connected to the tomb is blamed on the curse.
But Howard Carter, the man who opened the tomb, lives for another sixteen years, and dies in London in nineteen thirty nine, aged sixty four.
Today, the king's mask and treasures fill the halls of a new museum near the pyramids of Giza, visited by millions.
His ancient enemies chiselled his name off every wall, so that no one would remember him.
Three thousand years later, his is the one name everyone remembers.
