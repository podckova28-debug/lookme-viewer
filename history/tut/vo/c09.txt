In the early nineteen hundreds, many archaeologists believe the Valley of the Kings is empty.
Howard Carter disagrees.
He has found small objects with the name of a little-known king, Too-tan-kah-moon, and believes his tomb is still there.
For five seasons, paid by an English aristocrat, Lord Car-nar-von, he digs and finds almost nothing.
In nineteen twenty two, Car-nar-von tells him this is the last season.
On the fourth of November, under the remains of the old workmen's huts, his team uncovers a stone step.
Then another, and another.
Sixteen steps lead down to a sealed doorway.
Carter covers it up again and sends a telegram to England.
Three weeks later, Car-nar-von arrives, and Carter makes a hole in the second doorway, and holds up his candle.
And what he sees changes the history of archaeology.
