According to records found in the archives of the Hittites, Egypt's great rival in the north, an Egyptian queen, recently widowed, sends a letter to the Hittite king, Soo-pee-loo-lee-oo-mah.
My husband has died, she writes.
I have no son.
They say you have many sons.
Give me one of your sons, and he will become my husband.
I will never take a servant of mine and make him my husband.
The Hittite king cannot believe it.
He sends an envoy to check, and the queen writes again.
Finally, he sends a prince, named Zah-nahn-zah.
On the way to Egypt, the prince is killed.
No one knows by whom.
Most historians think the widow was Ahnk-eh-sen-ah-moon.
And the servant she refused to marry may have been the old courtier, Eye.
Because Eye is the man who now takes the throne.
