The Valley of the Kings, Egypt.
The twenty sixth of November, nineteen twenty two.
An English archaeologist named Howard Carter makes a small hole in an ancient plastered doorway, and holds a candle up to it.
Hot air from inside makes the flame flicker.
Behind him, the man paying for the dig asks: can you see anything?
Carter is silent for a long time.
Then he says: yes, wonderful things.
For three thousand years, the king who was buried here was one of the least important rulers Egypt ever had.
Today, he is the most famous.
This is the entire life of Too-tan-kah-moon, the boy king who did almost nothing, and the reason the whole world knows his name.
