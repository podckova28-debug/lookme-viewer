Around thirteen thirty two B C, at about eight or nine years old, Too-tan-kah-ten becomes pharaoh of Egypt.
He is handed the crook and the flail, the symbols of a king, and a wife: his half-sister, Ahnk-eh-sen-pah-ah-ten, a daughter of Ah-ken-ah-ten and the famous queen Neh-fer-tee-tee.
She is a few years older than him.
A child cannot rule an empire.
Real power sits with two older men.
Eye, a veteran courtier who served his father, and Hor-em-heb, the commander of the army.
And they have a plan for the young king.
Because Egypt is angry.
The old priests have lost their temples, the people have lost their gods, and the country blames the family of Ah-ken-ah-ten.
To survive, the boy will have to give up the one thing his father believed in.
