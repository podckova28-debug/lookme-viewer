Around thirteen twenty three B C, in about the ninth or tenth year of his reign, Too-tan-kah-moon dies, at roughly nineteen.
There is no record of how.
For decades, an old X-ray showing a bone fragment inside his skull convinced many people that he was murdered by a blow to the head.
Modern CT scans, in two thousand five, suggested something else.
The fragment probably came loose during mummification, and the king had a badly broken left leg, broken shortly before his death.
Some researchers think he fell from a chariot.
Others think a broken leg, together with malaria and a weak body, was enough to kill him.
The debate is still open.
What is certain is what happens next.
Egypt has no pharaoh, and no heir.
And the young widow, Ahnk-eh-sen-ah-moon, does something astonishing.
