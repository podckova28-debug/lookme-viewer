That kingdom is Kwah-razm, a Muslim empire stretching across Central Asia and Iran, ruled by Shah Muhammad.
In twelve eighteen, Jeng-giss Khan sends a caravan of about four hundred and fifty merchants, with silver, furs and silk.
At the border town of Oh-trar, the governor accuses them of spying and has almost all of them killed.
Jeng-giss Khan sends envoys to demand justice.
The Shah executes one of them, and sends the others back with their beards shaved off.
It is the worst decision of his life.
In twelve nineteen, the Mongol armies cross the mountains.
Boo-kha-rah, Samarkand, Oor-gench and Merv fall one after another.
Persian chroniclers claim that more than a million people died at Merv alone.
Historians think the real numbers were far lower, but some of these cities never recovered.
The Shah flees west, and dies alone on a small island in the Caspian Sea.
Jeng-giss Khan is now around sixty.
His sons Joh-chee and Chah-gah-tie have quarrelled in front of him over who should rule after him, and Chah-gah-tie has called Joh-chee a bastard.
And for the first time, he is afraid of something he cannot conquer.
