In twelve twenty six, he attacks the Western Shyah kingdom, in northwest China.
During a winter hunt, his horse is startled by wild horses, and he is thrown to the ground.
He keeps fighting through his injuries.
In August twelve twenty seven, in a camp in the mountains, Jeng-giss Khan dies.
No one knows exactly why: a fall, a fever, or an old wound.
His body is carried back to Mongolia, probably to the sacred mountain Boor-kahn Kal-doon.
Legend says the funeral escort kills everyone it meets on the road, and that horses trample the ground over the grave until no trace is left.
Expeditions with satellites and radar have searched for it.
It has never been found.
Today, geneticists estimate that around one in every two hundred men alive may descend from him or his family.
The boy who once hid in a river so that no one could find him became the most powerful man in the world.
And then he hid again.
This time, forever.
