To fight the Mer-kits, Teh-moo-jin needs warriors.
Tog-rool gives him some.
The rest come from Jah-moo-kha, a young chief who has been Teh-moo-jin's closest friend since childhood.
When they were eleven, the two boys swore to be ahn-dah, blood brothers, and swapped knucklebones on the frozen Oh-non River as tokens.
Together, Tog-rool, Jah-moo-kha and Teh-moo-jin attack the Mer-kits at night.
Teh-moo-jin rides through the fleeing crowd, calling Bor-teh's name, until she recognises his voice and runs to his horse.
Soon after, she gives birth to their first son, Joh-chee.
For the rest of his life, people will whisper about who his real father is.
For more than a year, Teh-moo-jin and Jah-moo-kha share a camp and a blanket.
But Jah-moo-kha believes in the old noble families.
Teh-moo-jin promotes anyone who is loyal, even shepherds.
One night, they ride in different directions.
And the steppe is about to choose between them.
