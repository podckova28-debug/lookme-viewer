Without a chief, Yeh-soo-gay's clan has no use for his widow and seven children.
One spring morning, the whole camp packs up and leaves, taking the herds with them.
Hoh-eh-loon and her children are abandoned on the steppe, where a family alone should not survive the winter.
Hoh-eh-loon ties up her skirt and digs for roots with a juniper stick.
The boys catch fish and marmots.
Teh-moo-jin grows up hungry, hard and watchful.
When he is about fourteen, his older half-brother, Beg-ter, keeps taking the food the younger boys catch.
One day he takes a fish.
Teh-moo-jin and his brother Kha-sar take their bows, and Beg-ter does not come home.
Hoh-eh-loon is furious.
She tells them they have no friends except their own shadows.
And very soon, the men who abandoned them come back for Teh-moo-jin.
