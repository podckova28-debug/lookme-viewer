Their friendship turns into a twenty-year war.
In eleven eighty seven, Jah-moo-kha defeats Teh-moo-jin in battle, and for years almost nothing is heard of him.
But Teh-moo-jin comes back, stronger.
He crushes the Tah-tars who poisoned his father.
Then, in twelve oh three, his oldest ally betrays him.
Tog-rool, worried about his own son's future, sets a trap.
Teh-moo-jin escapes with only a few men, and reaches a small, muddy lake called Bal-joo-nah.
There is nothing to eat but a wild horse they shoot, and nothing to drink but muddy water.
Teh-moo-jin raises the water to the sky and swears that if he ever wins, he will share everything with the men who drank it with him.
They become known as the Bal-joo-nah drinkers.
Within months, they strike back, and Tog-rool's Keh-rait army is destroyed.
Tog-rool flees, and is killed by border guards who do not recognise him.
Only one rival is left.
