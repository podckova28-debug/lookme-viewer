Mongolia, around eleven seventy seven.
A teenage boy is lying in a freezing river, with only his face above the water.
Around his neck is a heavy wooden collar, locked shut, so he cannot lift his hands to his mouth.
On the bank, in the moonlight, his enemies are searching for him.
He has no father, no army and no money.
Fifty years later, this boy will rule an empire more than twice the size of the Roman Empire at its peak.
And when he dies, his followers will make sure that no one, ever, can find his grave.
This is the entire life of Jeng-giss Khan, the outcast who conquered more land than any man before him, and the man who vanished at the end of it.
