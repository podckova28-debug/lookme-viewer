The Tie-chee-ood, the clan that left them, are afraid of what the boy might become.
They ride in, capture Teh-moo-jin, and lock his neck and wrists into a kang, a heavy wooden board that every captive must wear.
He is passed from tent to tent, a prisoner and a servant.
On the night of a summer feast, while his guards are drinking, Teh-moo-jin knocks out the one boy left to watch him and runs.
He hides in the river, collar and all, with only his face above the water.
A man named Sor-kahn Shee-rah, from another clan, sees him in the moonlight, and says nothing.
Later, Sor-kahn Shee-rah breaks the collar, burns the pieces, and hides Teh-moo-jin under a cart full of wool, while the searchers poke the wool with spears.
Teh-moo-jin escapes on a borrowed horse.
He is free.
But he still owns almost nothing, except a promise made to a girl when he was nine.
