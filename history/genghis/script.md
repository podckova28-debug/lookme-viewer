=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
Mongolia, around eleven seventy seven. A teenage boy is lying in a freezing river, with only his face above the water. Around his neck is a heavy wooden collar, locked shut, so he cannot lift his hands to his mouth. On the bank, in the moonlight, his enemies are searching for him. He has no father, no army and no money. Fifty years later, this boy will rule an empire more than twice the size of the Roman Empire at its peak. And when he dies, his followers will make sure that no one, ever, can find his grave. This is the entire life of Genghis Khan, the outcast who conquered more land than any man before him, and the man who vanished at the end of it.

=== c01 | The Blood Clot ===
He is born around eleven sixty two, beside the Onon River, in the grasslands of what is now Mongolia. His name is Temujin. According to The Secret History of the Mongols, he comes into the world clutching a clot of blood in his fist, and the old women say it means he will be a great warrior. His father, Yesugei, is a minor chief. His mother, Hoelun, was kidnapped by Yesugei from a Merkit warrior, on the way home from her own wedding. The Merkits will not forget it. When Temujin is nine, his father takes him to another clan to choose a bride. He is engaged to a girl named Borte, and left behind to live with her family. On the way home, Yesugei stops at a camp of Tatars, old enemies, who offer him food. A few days later, he is dead. They have poisoned him. And the boy is about to learn that his own people are no safer.

=== c02 | The Fish ===
Without a chief, Yesugei's clan has no use for his widow and seven children. One spring morning, the whole camp packs up and leaves, taking the herds with them. Hoelun and her children are abandoned on the steppe, where a family alone should not survive the winter. Hoelun ties up her skirt and digs for roots with a juniper stick. The boys catch fish and marmots. Temujin grows up hungry, hard and watchful. When he is about fourteen, his older half-brother, Begter, keeps taking the food the younger boys catch. One day he takes a fish. Temujin and his brother Khasar take their bows, and Begter does not come home. Hoelun is furious. She tells them they have no friends except their own shadows. And very soon, the men who abandoned them come back for Temujin.

=== c03 | The Cangue ===
The Tayichiud, the clan that left them, are afraid of what the boy might become. They ride in, capture Temujin, and lock his neck and wrists into a cangue, a heavy wooden board that every captive must wear. He is passed from tent to tent, a prisoner and a servant. On the night of a summer feast, while his guards are drinking, Temujin knocks out the one boy left to watch him and runs. He hides in the river, collar and all, with only his face above the water. A man named Sorkan Shira, from another clan, sees him in the moonlight, and says nothing. Later, Sorkan Shira breaks the collar, burns the pieces, and hides Temujin under a cart full of wool, while the searchers poke the wool with spears. Temujin escapes on a borrowed horse. He is free. But he still owns almost nothing, except a promise made to a girl when he was nine.

=== c04 | The Sable Coat ===
At sixteen, Temujin goes back for the girl he was promised to. Borte's family keeps their word, and her mother gives him a wedding gift: a coat of black sable fur, the most valuable thing he has ever owned. Temujin does not keep it. He rides to Toghrul, the powerful khan of the Kereit people, who was once his father's sworn brother, and gives him the coat. Toghrul accepts him like a son. It is the best investment Temujin will ever make. Because soon after, three hundred Merkit riders raid his camp at dawn. Eighteen years after Yesugei stole Hoelun from them, they take their revenge. They carry Borte away.

=== c05 | The Knucklebone ===
To fight the Merkits, Temujin needs warriors. Toghrul gives him some. The rest come from Jamukha, a young chief who has been Temujin's closest friend since childhood. When they were eleven, the two boys swore to be anda, blood brothers, and swapped knucklebones on the frozen Onon River as tokens. Together, Toghrul, Jamukha and Temujin attack the Merkits at night. Temujin rides through the fleeing crowd, calling Borte's name, until she recognises his voice and runs to his horse. Soon after, she gives birth to their first son, Jochi. For the rest of his life, people will whisper about who his real father is. For more than a year, Temujin and Jamukha share a camp and a blanket. But Jamukha believes in the old noble families. Temujin promotes anyone who is loyal, even shepherds. One night, they ride in different directions. And the steppe is about to choose between them.

=== c06 | The Muddy Water ===
Their friendship turns into a twenty-year war. In eleven eighty seven, Jamukha defeats Temujin in battle, and for years almost nothing is heard of him. But Temujin comes back, stronger. He crushes the Tatars who poisoned his father. Then, in twelve oh three, his oldest ally betrays him. Toghrul, worried about his own son's future, sets a trap. Temujin escapes with only a few men, and reaches a small, muddy lake called Baljuna. There is nothing to eat but a wild horse they shoot, and nothing to drink but muddy water. Temujin raises the water to the sky and swears that if he ever wins, he will share everything with the men who drank it with him. They become known as the Baljuna drinkers. Within months, they strike back, and Toghrul's Kereit army is destroyed. Toghrul flees, and is killed by border guards who do not recognise him. Only one rival is left.

=== c07 | The White Banner ===
In twelve oh four, Temujin defeats the Naimans, the last great tribe of the west. Jamukha, who fought with them, flees into the mountains with a handful of men. In twelve oh five, those men tie him up and hand him over, hoping for a reward. Temujin has the traitors executed for betraying their lord. Then, according to The Secret History, he offers his old blood brother friendship once more. Jamukha refuses, and asks only to die without his blood being spilled, as a noble. His wish is granted. In the spring of twelve oh six, at a great assembly by the Onon River, under a white banner with nine horse-tail tassels, Temujin is proclaimed ruler of all the people who live in felt tents. His new title is Chinggis Khan. Genghis Khan. And now, he looks south.

=== c08 | The Wall ===
In one battle, an enemy archer shoots Genghis Khan's horse from under him. When the archer is captured, he admits it to the khan's face, and expects to die. Instead, Genghis Khan gives him a new name, Jebe, the arrow, and makes him one of his greatest generals. That is how the new empire works. Loyalty and talent matter more than family. He splits his army into units of ten, a hundred, a thousand and ten thousand, mixing the tribes so that no clan can rebel, and gives them one law, the Yassa. In twelve eleven, he turns this army against the Jin Empire of northern China, a state of tens of millions of people, behind long defensive walls. At first, his horsemen cannot take walled cities. So they learn, from captured Chinese engineers. In twelve fifteen, the Jin capital, Zhongdu, where Beijing stands today, falls. Genghis Khan now rules from Manchuria to the Altai mountains. And he wants to trade with the richest kingdom to the west.

=== c09 | The Caravan ===
That kingdom is Khwarazm, a Muslim empire stretching across Central Asia and Iran, ruled by Shah Muhammad. In twelve eighteen, Genghis Khan sends a caravan of about four hundred and fifty merchants, with silver, furs and silk. At the border town of Otrar, the governor accuses them of spying and has almost all of them killed. Genghis Khan sends envoys to demand justice. The Shah executes one of them, and sends the others back with their beards shaved off. It is the worst decision of his life. In twelve nineteen, the Mongol armies cross the mountains. Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench and Merv fall one after another. Persian chroniclers claim that more than a million people died at Merv alone. Historians think the real numbers were far lower, but some of these cities never recovered. The Shah flees west, and dies alone on a small island in the Caspian Sea. Genghis Khan is now around sixty. His sons Jochi and Chagatai have quarrelled in front of him over who should rule after him, and Chagatai has called Jochi a bastard. And for the first time, he is afraid of something he cannot conquer.

=== c10 | The Elixir ===
He has heard of a famous Chinese Taoist monk, Changchun, who is said to be over three hundred years old. Genghis Khan sends for him, and the old monk travels for more than a year, across deserts and mountains, to reach the khan's camp in the Hindu Kush. In twelve twenty two, Genghis Khan asks him the question he has been waiting to ask. Do you have the medicine of immortality? The monk answers honestly. There are ways to protect life, he says, but there is no medicine that makes a man live forever. Genghis Khan thanks him, gives him gifts and frees his monasteries from taxes. Then he goes back to war. He has one more enemy, a kingdom that broke its promise to send him troops. And it will be his last campaign.

=== c11 | The Grave ===
In twelve twenty six, he attacks the Western Xia kingdom, in northwest China. During a winter hunt, his horse is startled by wild horses, and he is thrown to the ground. He keeps fighting through his injuries. In August twelve twenty seven, in a camp in the mountains, Genghis Khan dies. No one knows exactly why: a fall, a fever, or an old wound. His body is carried back to Mongolia, probably to the sacred mountain Burkhan Khaldun. Legend says the funeral escort kills everyone it meets on the road, and that horses trample the ground over the grave until no trace is left. Expeditions with satellites and radar have searched for it. It has never been found. Today, geneticists estimate that around one in every two hundred men alive may descend from him or his family. The boy who once hid in a river so that no one could find him became the most powerful man in the world. And then he hid again. This time, forever.
