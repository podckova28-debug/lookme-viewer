# The ENTIRE History of the Illuminati in 12 Minutes — script v1

Format: Archive-in-Minutes style (see ../FORMAT.md). Present tense, third person, no jokes, exact dates.
Cold open → thesis → chapters titled by an object → hook at the end of every chapter → one subscribe CTA (~3:40) → ending callback + aphorism.
Target ~1,850 words ≈ 12 min at ~155 wpm.

=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
In the summer of seventeen eighty-five, a priest named Johann Jakob Lanz is travelling across Bavaria when a storm breaks. Lightning strikes. Lanz is killed on the spot. According to a story told ever since, when the authorities search his body, they find papers sewn into his clothes. Names. Code words. Plans of a secret society that has already been banned. This is the entire history of the Illuminati, and how a club that lasted nine years became the most famous conspiracy in the world.

=== c01 | The Professor ===
On the first of May, seventeen seventy-six, in the Bavarian university town of Ingolstadt, a twenty-eight-year-old law professor named Adam Weishaupt meets four of his students. Weishaupt teaches canon law, the law of the Catholic Church. For almost two hundred years that job had belonged to the Jesuits. Weishaupt is the first man outside the order to hold it, and the Jesuits never forgive him. Bavaria in the seventeen seventies is one of the most Catholic states in Europe. Books are censored, and the clergy controls the schools. Weishaupt believes in the new ideas of the Enlightenment: reason, science, and a world run without kings and priests. He cannot say this in public. So he decides to build a society that can say it in secret. That night, five men found an order they call the Perfectibilists. Within two years, they choose a new name. The Illuminati. The enlightened ones. But a secret society needs secrets, and Weishaupt is about to invent a lot of them.

=== c02 | The Owl ===
Every member receives a code name from the ancient world. Weishaupt becomes Spartacus, after the slave who rose against Rome. Ingolstadt becomes Eleusis. Munich becomes Athens. Bavaria becomes Achaia. The order even has its own calendar, with Persian names for the months. Its symbol is the owl of Minerva, the Roman goddess of wisdom. New recruits start at the bottom, as Minervals. They report on their reading, their friends and their own weaknesses to superiors they have never met. Letters travel in cipher. Only the men at the top know who is really in charge. For four years, it barely works. By seventeen seventy-nine, the order has only a few dozen members, most of them students. Then a baron from northern Germany walks through the door, and everything changes.

=== c03 | The Baron ===
In seventeen eighty, Baron Adolph von Knigge joins the order under the name Philo. Knigge is a writer, a charmer and a well-connected Freemason. He sees what Weishaupt has missed. The Masonic lodges of Germany are full of educated, restless men looking for something more than dinners and ceremonies. Knigge rebuilds the Illuminati to look like a higher, secret level of Freemasonry, with new ranks and new rituals. Then he recruits inside the lodges. It works. By the mid seventeen eighties, the Illuminati have somewhere around two thousand members. Not students anymore. Professors, officials, doctors, nobles. The Duke of Gotha joins. The Duke of Weimar joins. And so does the most famous writer in Germany, Johann Wolfgang von Goethe. The order is growing faster than ever. And its two leaders are starting to hate each other.

=== c04 | The Quarrel ===
Knigge wants rituals, mystery and a little religion to keep the members loyal. Weishaupt wants control, and he does not like sharing it. He writes long letters full of orders. Knigge accuses him of acting like a Jesuit, the very thing the order was created to fight. In July seventeen eighty-four, Knigge resigns and walks away. He takes a lot of goodwill with him. By then, the order has a much bigger problem than a quarrel. People are talking. Former members tell stories about spying, secret tribunals and plans to take over the state. Some of it is exaggerated. Some of it is not. And the stories reach the one man in Bavaria who can end it all.

=== c05 | The Edict ===
Karl Theodor, the Elector of Bavaria, is a Catholic ruler who does not like surprises. On the twenty-second of June, seventeen eighty-four, he bans all secret societies that have not been approved by the state. The Illuminati try to carry on quietly. On the second of March, seventeen eighty-five, a second edict names them directly. The order is now illegal. Weishaupt is dismissed from his university. He flees Bavaria for the free city of Regensburg, and later finds protection in Gotha, with the duke who had joined his order. Members burn their letters. Officials lose their jobs. On paper, the Illuminati are finished. But the government still wants proof of what they were planning. And in the summer of seventeen eighty-five, the sky seems to hand it over.

=== c06 | The Lightning ===
Johann Jakob Lanz is a priest and, according to the story, a member of the order, riding as a messenger. Near Regensburg, in a thunderstorm, he is struck and killed. When his body is searched, the story goes, Illuminati documents are found in his clothes. Historians still argue about how much of this really happened, and what, if anything, was in his pockets. But the story spreads everywhere, and it gives the Bavarian police exactly what they need: a reason to keep looking. Fifteen months later, they find something real.

=== c07 | The Raid ===
In October seventeen eighty-six, officers search the house of Xaver von Zwackh, a government lawyer in Landshut. In the order, his name was Cato. He was one of Weishaupt's closest men. In his house, they find boxes of papers: membership lists, coded letters, the order's rules. They also find notes on secret ink, on a lock for hiding documents, and on recipes for a drink to end a pregnancy. In seventeen eighty-seven, the Bavarian government prints these papers as a book and sends copies to other courts and libraries across Europe. The idea is simple. Let everyone see what the Illuminati really were. It works too well. Now everyone in Europe has read about a secret society with code names, ciphers and members in high places. Two years later, in Paris, the world turns upside down.

=== c08 | The Revolution ===
In seventeen eighty-nine, the French Revolution begins. Kings, nobles and priests across Europe are terrified, and they want an explanation. How could the most powerful monarchy on the continent fall in a few years? In seventeen ninety-seven, two books give them one. In London, a Scottish scientist named John Robison publishes Proofs of a Conspiracy. In the same year, a French Jesuit priest, the Abbé Barruel, starts his Memoirs of Jacobinism. Both say the same thing. The Revolution was no accident. It was planned, by Freemasons and by the Illuminati. There is very little evidence that the order played any real part. It had been dead for years before the Bastille fell. But the books become bestsellers. And one of them crosses the Atlantic.

=== c09 | The Letter ===
In seventeen ninety-eight, a Boston minister named Jedidiah Morse reads Robison's book and starts preaching that the Illuminati are at work in the United States. Newspapers pick it up. For a few months, New England is gripped by an Illuminati scare. Someone sends a copy of Robison's book to the most respected man in America, George Washington. In October seventeen ninety-eight, Washington writes back. He does not believe American Freemasons are spreading these ideas. But he writes that he has no doubt the doctrines of the Illuminati have spread into the United States. A former president, writing about a German secret society that has been banned for thirteen years. The scare fades. But the name has now taken root in America. And so has a symbol that will later be tied to it.

=== c10 | The Pyramid ===
In seventeen eighty-two, the United States Congress approves a Great Seal for the new nation. On its reverse side, there is an unfinished pyramid, an eye inside a triangle at the top, and at the base, in Roman numerals, the year seventeen seventy-six. The year of American independence. It is also, by coincidence, the year Weishaupt founded his order. The eye is an old Christian symbol, the Eye of Providence, and the designers never mention the Illuminati. Then, in nineteen thirty-five, the pyramid and the eye are printed on the back of the one-dollar bill. Billions of copies. In every pocket in America. All the myth needs now is someone to tell the story again.

=== c11 | The Novel ===
In the nineteen sixties and seventies, two American writers, Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson, work at a men's magazine that receives piles of letters from conspiracy believers. They decide to turn them into fiction. In nineteen seventy-five, they publish a three-part novel, The Illuminatus! Trilogy. In their story, the Illuminati are behind everything, from assassinations to rock music. It is meant partly as a joke. Many readers do not take it as one. Then come films, video games, comic books, and finally the internet. By the twenty-first century, the Illuminati are blamed for wars, banks, elections, and the careers of pop stars. Almost none of the people who believe it have heard the name Adam Weishaupt.

=== c12 | The End ===
Weishaupt himself lives quietly in Gotha for more than forty years. He writes books defending himself and the order. He never brings it back. He dies on the eighteenth of November, eighteen thirty, at the age of eighty-two. No secret ceremony. No hidden successor. In eighteen ninety-six, a German writer named Leopold Engel starts a new order using the old name. It stays small and fades away. And the papers that the Bavarian police seized in seventeen eighty-six can still be read today, in German archives. They show a club of professors, students and officials who used code names, wrote in cipher, and argued about who was in charge. The Illuminati lasted nine years. The fear of them has lasted two hundred and fifty.

## Notes and sources to verify before recording
- Founded 1 May 1776, Ingolstadt; Weishaupt (b. 6 Feb 1748) — first lay professor of canon law there (chair previously held by Jesuits; Jesuit order suppressed 1773). First name "Bund der Perfektibilisten"; renamed Illuminatenorden ~1778.
- Code names: Weishaupt = Spartacus, Knigge = Philo, Zwackh = Cato; Ingolstadt = Eleusis, Munich = Athens, Bavaria = Achaia; Persian calendar; owl of Minerva; Minerval grade.
- Knigge joined 1780, reorganised with Masonic degrees; left July 1784 (formal agreement of departure).
- Membership ~2,000 (estimates 2,000–2,500). Ernst II of Saxe-Gotha-Altenburg, Carl August of Saxe-Weimar, Goethe (1783, code name Abaris), Herder.
- Edicts of Karl Theodor: 22 June 1784 (unauthorised societies), 2 March 1785 (Illuminati and Freemasons named), 16 Aug 1787 (harsher).
- Lanz lightning story (1785, near Regensburg): treat as legend — script hedges ("according to the story").
- Zwackh house search, Landshut, Oct 1786; "Einige Originalschriften des Illuminatenordens" printed 1787.
- Robison, Proofs of a Conspiracy (1797); Barruel, Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire du jacobinisme (1797–98).
- Jedidiah Morse sermons 1798; Washington to G. W. Snyder, 24 Oct 1798 (doctrines of the Illuminati spread in the US; did not believe lodges were contaminated).
