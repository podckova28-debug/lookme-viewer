=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
It is past midnight in Saint Petersburg, at the end of December nineteen sixteen. In the basement of a palace on the Moika canal, a bearded peasant from Siberia is offered small pink cakes and a glass of sweet wine. According to the man who invited him, both are laced with enough cyanide to kill several people. He eats the cakes. He drinks the wine. And nothing happens. This is the entire life of Grigori Rasputin, the peasant who came within reach of an empire, and the man his enemies could not seem to kill.

=== c01 | The Village ===
He is born on the twenty-first of January, eighteen sixty-nine, in Pokrovskoye, a village of wooden houses on the Tura River in western Siberia. His father is a peasant farmer and coachman. Grigori never goes to school, and for most of his life he can barely read or write. As a young man he has a bad reputation in the village. He drinks. He fights. Neighbours accuse him of stealing horses. At eighteen, he marries a peasant girl named Praskovia, and they will have seven children, though only three survive. It looks like the life of a thousand other Siberian peasants. Then, in his late twenties, he walks out of the village and does not come back for months.

=== c02 | The Road ===
In eighteen ninety-seven, Rasputin goes on a pilgrimage to the monastery of Verkhoturye, more than four hundred kilometres away. He returns a changed man. He stops drinking and stops eating meat. He prays for hours and talks about God to anyone who will listen. He never becomes a priest or a monk. Instead, he becomes a strannik, a wandering pilgrim, walking thousands of kilometres across Russia, to holy places, monasteries and shrines. People begin to call him a starets, a holy elder. They say he can read souls, predict the future and heal the sick with prayer. In a country where most people are peasants and deeply religious, that is a powerful reputation. And it is about to carry him all the way to the capital.

=== c03 | The Capital ===
Around nineteen hundred and four, Rasputin arrives in Saint Petersburg, the imperial capital. He is dirty, long-haired, and speaks like a peasant, and high society is fascinated. Priests introduce him to their friends. Two princesses from Montenegro, married into the imperial family, introduce him to theirs. On the first of November, nineteen oh five, Rasputin meets Tsar Nicholas the Second and Empress Alexandra. That evening, the Tsar writes in his diary that they have met a man of God, named Grigori, from the province of Tobolsk. To most of Russia, the visit means nothing. But inside the palace, the imperial family is hiding a secret that will make this peasant one of the most important people in the empire.

=== c04 | The Secret ===
In nineteen oh four, after four daughters, the Empress finally gives birth to a son and heir, Alexei. Within weeks, the doctors find blood that will not stop. Alexei has haemophilia, a disease in which the blood does not clot. A bruise can become a swelling. A fall can kill him. There is no cure. The family decides to keep the illness a state secret. The public is not told. Even most ministers are not told. So when Alexei suffers, his mother suffers alone, and she is desperate for anyone who can help. Rasputin is invited to the boy's bedside. He prays, he talks to him softly, and the attacks seem to pass. To Alexandra, this is a miracle. To everyone else at court, the peasant is now far too close to the throne.

=== c05 | The Telegram ===
In October nineteen twelve, the imperial family is staying at a hunting lodge at Spała, in Russian Poland. Alexei falls during a carriage ride, and a huge bleed swells inside his thigh and stomach. For days he screams with pain. The doctors give up. The family announces his condition in official bulletins, and a priest gives him the last rites. In despair, Alexandra sends a telegram to Rasputin in his Siberian village. His answer comes back almost at once. God has seen your tears and heard your prayers. The little one will not die. Do not let the doctors bother him too much. The next day, the bleeding begins to stop. Doctors still argue about why. For Alexandra, there is no argument left. Rasputin can save her son, and she will defend him against anyone. Two years later, someone tries to kill him.

=== c06 | The Knife ===
On the twelfth of July, nineteen fourteen, back in Pokrovskoye, a woman with a covered face approaches Rasputin in the street. Her name is Khioniya Guseva. She is a follower of a monk who has become his bitter enemy. She pulls a knife and stabs him in the stomach. Rasputin runs, bleeding, and survives. For weeks, he lies in hospital in Tyumen. And while he lies there, Europe goes to war. Rasputin begs the Tsar by telegram not to fight, warning that the war will destroy Russia. Nicholas ignores him. Within a year, millions of Russian soldiers are dead, wounded or captured, and the Tsar makes a decision that hands the peasant even more power.

=== c07 | The Throne Room ===
In September nineteen fifteen, Nicholas leaves the capital to command the army himself at the front. At home, the Empress is left in charge of the government. And the Empress listens to Rasputin. Ministers who criticise him are dismissed. People he recommends are appointed. In two years, Russia goes through four prime ministers, and gossip blames almost every change on the peasant. Rumours spread through the cities and the trenches. That he controls the Empress. That she is his lover. That both of them are working for Germany, because Alexandra was born a German princess. Most of it is not true. But people believe it. Cartoons show the Tsar and Tsarina as puppets on Rasputin's strings. And a group of men inside the imperial family decide that the only way to save the monarchy is to kill him.

=== c08 | The Cakes ===
The plot is led by Prince Felix Yusupov, one of the richest men in Russia and married to the Tsar's niece. His partners are Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich, the Tsar's cousin, and Vladimir Purishkevich, a right-wing member of parliament. Yusupov invites Rasputin to his palace on the Moika on the night of the twenty-ninth of December, nineteen sixteen, promising to introduce him to his beautiful wife. She is not even in the city. In the basement, a doctor in the plot has put cyanide in the cakes and the wine. At least, that is what Yusupov later claims. Rasputin eats and drinks for more than two hours. He sings. He asks for more. Upstairs, the other plotters wait, listening to a gramophone playing the same song again and again. Finally, Yusupov takes a pistol, goes back down the stairs, and shoots Rasputin in the chest.

=== c09 | The Bridge ===
Rasputin collapses. The plotters celebrate. Then, according to Yusupov, the dead man opens his eyes, grabs him by the shoulder, and staggers out into the snowy courtyard. Purishkevich runs after him and fires. Rasputin falls in the snow. The killers wrap his body in cloth, drive to the Petrovsky Bridge, and throw it through a hole in the ice of the Malaya Nevka river. On the first of January, nineteen seventeen, police find the body frozen under the ice. The autopsy finds three gunshot wounds. The last one is a shot to the forehead, at close range. There is no sign of poison. For a century, people will argue about what really happened in that basement, and some historians even suspect British intelligence of helping the plotters. The Empress buries him in secret near the palace. And the killers expect to be thanked.

=== c10 | The Letter ===
Instead, the empire they wanted to save collapses. In March nineteen seventeen, less than three months after the murder, revolution breaks out in Petrograd and Nicholas gives up the throne. Revolutionary soldiers find Rasputin's grave, dig up the body and burn it. A letter said to be written by Rasputin begins to circulate. In it, he warns the Tsar that if he is killed by the Tsar's own relatives, none of the family will survive for more than two years. Historians doubt the letter is real. But on the seventeenth of July, nineteen eighteen, nineteen months after Rasputin's death, Nicholas, Alexandra, Alexei and his four sisters are shot by the Bolsheviks in a cellar in Yekaterinburg. Felix Yusupov escapes abroad with his family and lives in Paris until nineteen sixty-seven, telling the story of the night he killed Rasputin for the rest of his life.

=== c11 | The Song ===
Yusupov's version, with the poison that did not work and the dead man who stood up, becomes the legend. Books, films and cartoons turn Rasputin into a mad monk with hypnotic eyes, although he was never a monk. In nineteen seventy-eight, a disco band called Boney M sings about him, and the song becomes a hit around the world. Today, millions of people know his name, and almost none of them know about the sick boy he was called to help. Rasputin was a peasant who could barely write. He never held an office, commanded an army or signed a law. And yet, more than a hundred years later, he is still the most famous Russian of the last days of the empire. The poison did not kill him. The legend never will.
