# The Entire Life of CLEOPATRA — Food & Drinks (ketchup/Napoleon) format

Running gag: a Roman propaganda scribe (Octavian's poet) keeps writing a scroll titled "THE EVIL EGYPTIAN QUEEN"; payoff at the end — she wasn't even Egyptian, and history was written by the man who won.
Second-person "you" scenes (stick figure as a Roman legionary). One sentence per line. Numbers spelled out for TTS.

=== p0 ===
You are a Roman soldier.
It's August, thirty BC, and you are standing outside a tomb in Alexandria, in Egypt.
Inside, the most famous woman in the world has locked herself in with her gold, her jewels, and two servants.
Her name is Cleopatra.
Her lover is dead.
Her navy is gone.
And your boss, Octavian, wants to parade her through Rome in chains.
You know three things about her.
She was Egyptian.
She was the most beautiful woman alive.
And she died from the bite of a snake.
The first one is wrong.
The second one, nobody really knows.
And the third one, historians still argue about.
So who was she really?
And why does everything we think we know about her come from the man standing behind you?
Spoiler.
He has a writer.
And the writer is very busy.

=== p1 ===
Let's go back to the beginning.
Cleopatra is born around sixty nine BC, in Alexandria, the richest city on the Mediterranean.
Her family has ruled Egypt for almost three hundred years.
But they are not Egyptian.
They are Greek.
When Alexander the Great died, one of his generals, Ptolemy, grabbed Egypt for himself.
Every king after him is called Ptolemy.
Every queen is called Cleopatra, Berenice or Arsinoe.
Family reunions were very confusing.
She is Cleopatra the seventh.
The family has a tradition.
Brothers marry sisters, to keep the throne in the family.
And they also murder each other.
A lot.
Cleopatra is the first of her family in three hundred years to bother learning Egyptian.
She also speaks Greek, Hebrew, Aramaic and several other languages.
Plutarch says her voice was like an instrument with many strings.
So she is clever.
She will need to be.

=== p2 ===
In fifty one BC, her father dies.
Cleopatra is eighteen.
His will says she must rule together with her brother, Ptolemy the thirteenth.
He is ten.
So naturally, she marries him.
It goes about as well as you'd expect.
The boy's advisers hate her.
Within three years, they throw her out of Alexandria.
She flees to Syria, raises an army, and waits at the border.
Then, in forty eight BC, the most powerful man in the world sails into Alexandria.
Julius Caesar.
He's chasing his rival, Pompey.
The boy king's advisers want to impress him.
So they cut off Pompey's head and hand it to Caesar.
Guess who had to carry the platter. You.
Caesar is not impressed.
And Cleopatra realises something.
Whoever gets to Caesar first wins Egypt.
There's just one problem.
Her brother's army is guarding the whole city.

=== p3 ===
So she gets smuggled in.
The story says she was rolled up in a carpet, or a bedding sack, and carried into the palace by a single servant.
The servant unrolls it in front of Caesar.
And out comes the queen of Egypt.
From now on, Caesar checks every carpet in Egypt before he sits down.
Caesar is fifty two.
She is twenty one.
By the next morning, they are allies.
And a lot more than allies.
Her brother is furious, and starts a war against both of them.
During the fighting, part of the famous Library of Alexandria burns.
The brother drowns in the Nile, trying to escape in heavy armour.
Cleopatra is queen again.
She marries her other brother, who is twelve, just for the paperwork.
Romantic.
And a few months later, she has a son.
She calls him Caesarion.
Little Caesar.
Back in Rome, a man named Octavian, Caesar's great nephew, takes note.

=== p4 ===
In forty six BC, Cleopatra goes to Rome.
Caesar puts her up in his villa across the Tiber.
He even puts a golden statue of her in a temple.
The Romans are horrified.
A foreign queen.
In Rome.
With a gold statue.
Guess who has to guard the statue. You. Nobody tells you why.
Then, on the Ides of March, forty four BC, Caesar is stabbed to death in the Senate.
Twenty three wounds.
His will names his heir.
And it isn't Caesarion.
It's Octavian.
Cleopatra leaves Rome fast.
Back home, her little brother husband suddenly dies.
Many historians think she had him poisoned.
Now she rules with her son, who is three.
Much easier to work with.

=== p5 ===
Rome is now split between Octavian in the west and Caesar's general, Mark Antony, in the east.
In forty one BC, Antony summons Cleopatra to the city of Tarsus, to explain herself.
She arrives on a golden barge, with purple sails and silver oars, dressed as the goddess Aphrodite.
Antony was expecting an apology.
Instead, he gets a party.
For days.
There's a story that she bet him she could spend ten million sesterces on a single dinner.
Then she dropped a giant pearl into a cup of vinegar, let it dissolve, and drank it.
Scientists have tested this.
It takes a very long time, and very strong vinegar.
But it makes a great story.
Antony follows her back to Alexandria.
They form a drinking club called the Inimitable Livers.
You are not invited. Somebody has to stay sober and hold the shields.
And in forty BC, she has twins.
A boy and a girl, the Sun and the Moon.
In Rome, Octavian's writer sharpens his pen.

=== p6 ===
Antony marries Octavian's sister, to keep the peace.
Then he goes back to Cleopatra anyway.
In thirty four BC, in Alexandria, Antony holds a huge ceremony.
He gives Cleopatra and her children whole kingdoms of the Roman east.
For Octavian, it is the perfect gift.
He tells Rome that Antony has been bewitched by an Egyptian sorceress.
He reads out Antony's will in the Senate, saying Antony wants to be buried in Alexandria.
He doesn't even declare war on Antony.
He declares war on Cleopatra.
A foreign queen is a much easier enemy than a Roman general.
His poets get to work.
They call her a harlot queen, a drunk, a monster from the Nile.
None of them ever met her.
But they were paid by the word.

=== p7 ===
On the second of September, thirty one BC, the two fleets meet at Actium, off the coast of Greece.
In the middle of the battle, Cleopatra's sixty ships suddenly break through and sail away.
Antony leaves the fight and follows her.
His army, abandoned, surrenders.
You are in that army, by the way. Nobody told you anything. Again.
Nobody knows exactly why she left.
A planned escape, panic, or a wind change.
Octavian's writers know exactly why.
A coward, they say.
Underlined three times.
A year later, Octavian arrives at Alexandria.
Antony hears a false report that Cleopatra is dead.
He falls on his sword.
He doesn't die right away, and he's carried to her tomb, where she pulls him up through a window with ropes.
He dies in her arms.
And Cleopatra is captured.

=== p8 ===
Octavian wants her alive, for his triumph in Rome.
She has seen what happens to captured queens in a Roman parade.
On the tenth of August, thirty BC, her servants bring in a basket of figs.
A little later, Cleopatra is found dead on a golden couch, dressed as a queen.
She is thirty nine.
Was there a snake in the basket?
The ancient writers themselves weren't sure.
An Egyptian cobra is almost two metres long, which is a big snake to hide among figs.
Many historians now think she used poison.
Octavian has Caesarion killed.
Two Caesars is one too many.
Egypt becomes a Roman province, and stays one for six hundred years.
And Octavian becomes Augustus, the first emperor of Rome.
Which means his version of Cleopatra became the official one.
The seductress.
The Egyptian.
The woman who ruined Rome's best men.
She wasn't Egyptian.
She was a Greek queen who spoke nine languages, ran the richest country in the world for twenty one years, and outplayed Rome for two decades.
Her tomb has never been found.
Maybe someone should check the carpets.
If you liked this story, subscribe, there's a new life every week.
So, genius or villain?
Tell me in the comments.

## Facts to keep straight
- Born c. 69 BC (late 70/early 69), Alexandria. Ptolemaic dynasty founded by Ptolemy I Soter (Alexander's general), 305 BC; ~300 years by her time.
- Plutarch, Life of Antony 27: first Ptolemy to learn Egyptian; languages incl. Ethiopians, Troglodytes, Hebrews, Arabs, Syrians, Medes, Parthians; voice like a many-stringed instrument.
- Ptolemy XII died 51 BC; Cleopatra ~18; co-ruler Ptolemy XIII ~10. Expelled 49/48 BC, raised army in Syria. Pompey killed Sept 48 BC; head presented to Caesar.
- Smuggled in by Apollodorus the Sicilian in a bedsack (Plutarch: "stromatodesmon") — carpet is the popular version. Caesar b. 100 BC (52), she 21.
- Alexandrian War; part of the Library/warehouses burned (extent debated). Ptolemy XIII drowned in the Nile Jan 47 BC. Married Ptolemy XIV (~12). Caesarion b. June 47 BC.
- Rome 46–44 BC, Caesar's gardens across the Tiber; gilded statue in temple of Venus Genetrix. Ides of March 44 BC, 23 wounds (Suetonius). Heir: Octavian. Ptolemy XIV died 44 BC, poisoning suspected (Josephus).
- Tarsus 41 BC: barge with gilded stern, purple sails, silver oars, dressed as Aphrodite (Plutarch). Pearl wager: Pliny, Natural History 9.119–121 (10 million sesterces). "Inimitable Livers" (Amimetobioi). Twins Alexander Helios and Cleopatra Selene, 40 BC.
- Antony married Octavia 40 BC. Donations of Alexandria 34 BC. Octavian read Antony's will (burial in Alexandria) 32 BC; war declared on Cleopatra only.
- Actium 2 Sept 31 BC; Cleopatra's ~60 ships broke out. Antony's suicide Aug 30 BC after false report; hoisted into the mausoleum by ropes (Plutarch).
- Death ~10 or 12 Aug 30 BC, aged 39; figs basket; Plutarch & Cassius Dio both say the cause is unknown. Egyptian cobra up to ~2 m. Caesarion killed ("too many Caesars is not good", Plutarch quoting Arius). Egypt a Roman province until the Arab conquest (641 AD). Augustus from 27 BC. Reign 51–30 BC = 21 years. Tomb not found.
- "nine languages": Plutarch's list implies ~9 incl. Greek/Egyptian; phrased as "spoke nine languages" — common reading.
