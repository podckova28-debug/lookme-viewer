=== c00 | COLD OPEN ===
April, sixteen twelve. A tiny island in the strait between the main island of Japan and Kyushu. On the beach, the most famous swordsman in the country is waiting, with a sword so long that people call it the Drying Pole. His opponent is late. One hour. Two hours. When the boat finally lands, the man who steps out is not carrying a sword at all. He is carrying a wooden oar that he carved into a blade during the crossing. A few seconds later, the champion is lying on the sand. This is the entire life of Miyamoto Musashi, the man who fought more than sixty duels and never lost one, and then wrote a book about it that people still buy today.

=== c01 | The Placard ===
He is born around fifteen eighty four, in the province of Harima, in western Japan, at the end of a century of civil war. His father is a samurai and a master of the sword and the jitte, a short iron truncheon. He is said to be a hard, cold man, and the boy grows up big, wild and difficult. At thirteen, he sees a wooden placard in a village. A travelling swordsman named Arima Kihei has put it up, challenging anyone who dares to fight him. The boy writes his name on it. Kihei is a trained adult samurai. Musashi is a teenager with a wooden staff. When they meet, Musashi throws him to the ground and beats him until he does not get up. It is the first duel of his life. And from now on, he will never stop looking for the next one.

=== c02 | The Battlefield ===
In sixteen hundred, the two great armies of Japan meet at Sekigahara, in the biggest battle the country has ever seen. Legend says the young Musashi is there, on the losing side, and walks away alive. Some historians think he was actually fighting somewhere else, on the island of Kyushu. Either way, the war ends with a new ruler, Tokugawa Ieyasu, and a new problem for men like Musashi. Without war, a samurai without a lord is a ronin, a masterless man. Musashi has no lord, no money and no school. So he does what ronin do. He walks. From province to province, sleeping rough, training alone, and challenging the best swordsmen he can find. In sixteen oh four, at about twenty, he arrives in the capital, Kyoto. And he picks a fight with the most respected sword school in Japan.

=== c03 | The Pine Tree ===
The Yoshioka family have taught swordsmanship to the shoguns for generations. Musashi challenges the head of the school, Yoshioka Seijuro. He arrives late, to make his opponent nervous. Then he knocks him down with one blow of a wooden sword. Seijuro survives, but his honour does not. His younger brother, Denshichiro, challenges Musashi to avenge him. Musashi kills him. Now the Yoshioka want revenge, and they stop pretending it is a duel. They name the head of the family, a boy of about twelve, as the official challenger, and they meet at a pine tree outside the city, with dozens of armed students hiding nearby. This time, Musashi comes early. He waits in the dark. When they arrive, he steps out, kills the boy first, and cuts his way out through the crowd. The Yoshioka school never recovers. And Musashi learns something he will write down forty years later: in a fight, the rules are whatever you can get away with.

=== c04 | The Wooden Sword ===
For the next ten years, Musashi wanders and fights. A man with a chain and sickle. Men with spears. Men with staffs. According to the stories, he wins against all of them, and more and more, he does it with a wooden sword. Steel is for killing. Wood, he seems to say, is enough to win. He also starts to fight with two swords at once, a long one and a short one, which almost nobody does. By his own count, between the ages of thirteen and twenty nine, he fights more than sixty times, and he is never defeated. But one name keeps coming up wherever he goes. Sasaki Kojiro. A swordsman famous for a cut so fast, they say it could knock a swallow out of the air.

=== c05 | The Oar ===
The duel is arranged for the thirteenth of April, sixteen twelve, on the small island of Funajima, near the port of Kokura. Kojiro arrives on time, with his long sword, the Drying Pole. Musashi does not. He sleeps late. On the boat, he takes a spare oar and carves it into a wooden sword, longer than Kojiro's blade. When he finally jumps into the shallow water, hours late, Kojiro is furious. He draws his sword and throws the scabbard into the sea. And Musashi, according to the legend, says: Kojiro, you have already lost. A man who expects to win would never throw away his scabbard. They strike at almost the same moment. Kojiro's blade cuts through the cloth around Musashi's head. Musashi's oar crushes Kojiro's skull. The island is renamed after the loser. Ganryujima. Musashi is about twenty eight years old, and the most famous swordsman in Japan. And after this day, he never fights to the death in a duel again.

=== c06 | The Stone ===
Instead, Musashi looks for a place in the new world of peace. In sixteen fourteen and sixteen fifteen, he serves with the army of the shogun at the siege of Osaka Castle, the last great battle of the age. He adopts sons and finds them good positions in noble houses. One of them, Iori, becomes a senior official of a powerful lord. But Musashi himself never gets the high post he wants. In sixteen thirty eight, now in his fifties, he joins one last war, against rebels holding a fortress at Shimabara. And the man no swordsman could beat is knocked down by a rock, thrown from the castle wall, which hurts his legs so badly he cannot stand. He writes about it in a letter, almost embarrassed. The swordsman's war is over. And he is about to pick up a different kind of weapon.

=== c07 | The Brush ===
In his last years, Musashi turns to art. He paints in black ink. Birds on bare branches, monks, wild geese. One of his paintings, a shrike sitting on a dead branch, is now an official Important Cultural Property of Japan. He writes calligraphy, carves wood and even designs gardens. He says the same principle that wins a sword fight can be used in any art: see clearly, waste nothing, strike once. In sixteen forty, the lord of Kumamoto, Hosokawa Tadatoshi, invites him to live in his domain as an honoured guest. He is given a house, a salary, and finally respect. But a year later, his patron dies, and Musashi walks away from people one last time.

=== c08 | The Cave ===
In sixteen forty three, he goes to Reigando, a cave in the mountains near Kumamoto. There, sitting on the rock floor, he starts to write. The book is called Go Rin no Sho. The Book of Five Rings. Five chapters, named Earth, Water, Fire, Wind and Void. It explains how to hold a sword, how to stand, how to read an enemy, how to make him angry, how to win. A week before he dies, he writes one last page of rules for himself. Twenty one lines. Accept everything just the way it is. Do not regret what you have done. Never stray from the way. On the thirteenth of June, sixteen forty five, at about sixty, Miyamoto Musashi dies, probably of cancer. Legend says he is buried in full armour, beside the road, so that he can keep watch for his lord forever. He thought his book was a manual for swordsmen. He was wrong.

=== c09 | The Book ===
Three hundred years later, Japan has no more samurai. But in the nineteen thirties, a novel about Musashi becomes a national bestseller, and turns him into a hero for millions. In the nineteen seventies, The Book of Five Rings is translated into English. And business people start buying it, as a guide to competition and strategy. Today it sells in dozens of languages, next to books about money and management. The boy who wrote his name on a stranger's placard, the man who beat a champion with an oar, now sits on airport bookshelves around the world. He fought more than sixty men with a sword. The book he wrote in a cave has defeated all of them.
